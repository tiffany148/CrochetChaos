Most crochet books, videos and charts are made by right-handed crocheters, but crochet itself works just as well in the left hand. Nothing about the stitches changes: a left-handed single crochet has the same loops, the same height and the same count as a right-handed one. What changes is the direction you travel and the way pictures in books look to you. This chapter collects what left-handed crocheters most often need and what right-handed teachers need to know when they teach them.

> [!STATUS]
> This chapter describes standard practice. It makes no claim about which hand any person should use; crochet with whichever hand feels natural, and change if it does not.

## What is the same and what is mirrored

| Element | Right-handed | Left-handed |
|---|---|---|
| Hook hand | Right | Left |
| Yarn (tension) hand | Left | Right |
| Direction along a row | Right to left | Left to right |
| Direction around a round (seen from the right side) | Counterclockwise | Clockwise |
| Stitch anatomy, loop counts, heights | Same | Same |
| Written US or UK instructions | Same words | Same words |
| Right side and wrong side of the fabric | Same rules | Same rules |
| Diagrams, photos and charts | As printed | Usually need mirroring |

Because written instructions only name stitches and where they go, a left-handed crocheter can follow almost any written pattern exactly as printed. "Sc in the next 3 sts" means the next three stitches in the direction you are traveling.

## Learning from right-handed pictures

Photographs and drawings are the hardest part. Three practical ways to use them:

- **Mirror the image.** Most phones and computers can flip a photo horizontally. A flipped right-handed photo is a correct left-handed view.
- **Use a mirror.** Stand a small mirror beside a printed diagram, at a right angle to the page, and look at the reflection.
- **Sit facing the teacher.** In person, a left-handed learner sitting opposite a right-handed teacher sees the work roughly as a mirror image, which is what they need.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Flip the video, not your brain
> Many video players and editing apps have a mirror or flip option. Trying to reverse every move in your head while learning a new stitch doubles the difficulty for no reason.

## Charts for left-handed crocheters

A crochet chart is a map of the right side of the fabric. Left-handed crocheters read it in the mirror direction:

- **Rows:** right-handed rows on the right side of the fabric start at the right edge. Left-handed rows on the right side start at the left edge. On a chart, the row numbers show where a right-handed row starts; a left-handed crocheter begins at the opposite end of the same row.
- **Rounds:** right-handed charts in the round travel counterclockwise. Left-handed crocheters travel clockwise, starting from the same place.
- **Asymmetric motifs:** a chart with letters or a picture that faces one way will appear mirrored when worked left-handed exactly as charted. For letters and numbers, mirror the chart first.

> [!HINT] Mirror a chart on paper or screen
> Copy the chart and flip it horizontally. The mirrored chart can then be read like a right-handed one, starting from the side where its numbers now sit. For tapestry and C2C letters, always test-read a mirrored chart before stitching a name.

## Letters, words and directional pictures

Most motifs are symmetric and do not care which hand made them. Words do. If you work a tapestry or C2C name left-handed from a chart drawn for right-handed crochet, the result can read backward. The fix is the same as above: mirror the chart before you start. Text worked as surface crochet or embroidered after the fabric is made is not affected.

## Patterns with a built-in direction

A few things in a pattern do depend on the direction of travel:

- **Spiral rounds** lean in the direction you work. Left-handed amigurumi spirals lean the other way; this is a mirror, not an error.
- **Left and right pieces.** A pattern that says "right front" or "left mitt" may shape the piece based on how it lies when worked. Left-handed work produces the mirror piece, so a left-handed crocheter may need to follow the other piece's instructions, or check the shaping against a schematic.
- **Twisted or slanted stitches** (crossed stitches, herringbone, some cables) slant the opposite way. They still look correct as fabric.
- **Buttonholes** placed on "the right front for women's garments" are placed by the finished garment, not by the crochet direction; check the schematic, not the row direction.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Check pairs against the schematic
> When a pattern has a left piece and a right piece, lay your first piece on the schematic before starting the second. You will see immediately whether to follow the same or the opposite instructions.

## Holding the hook and yarn

The holds in Chapter 9 work in either hand. Choose a pencil or knife hold with the hook in your left hand and tension the yarn over the fingers of your right hand. As for right-handed learners, there is no single correct hold: the right one produces even loops without pain.

## Teaching a left-handed beginner

- Teach the same vocabulary: loops, posts, tops, turning chains. Anatomy does not mirror.
- Demonstrate slowly, sitting opposite, or film your hands and flip the video.
- Expect the first row to travel left to right and praise it.
- Offer mirrored diagrams of the first stitches and the magic ring.
- Do not ask a natural left-hander to switch hands unless they want to; let them try both if they are unsure.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> I have taught crochet to more left-handers than I can count, and every one of them learned it faster once I stopped trying to make them copy me and started sitting across the table.

## Quick reference for left-handed crocheters

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Written row instructions | Follow as printed |
| Written round instructions | Follow as printed; travel clockwise |
| Photo or drawing | Flip it horizontally |
| Chart in rows | Start each right-side row at the left edge |
| Chart in rounds | Travel clockwise from the same start |
| Letters or directional images | Mirror the chart before you begin |
| Left and right pieces | Check against the schematic before the second piece |
| Spiral leaning the other way | Normal mirror image |
