This chapter puts the pattern-reading rules from Chapters 13 and 14 to work. Each example gives a real-style pattern line, what it means in plain words, and the count check that proves you read it right. The counts were checked by arithmetic.

> [!HINT] The universal check
> For any row or round: **stitches used from the row below** must equal the stitches available, and **stitches made** must equal the count printed at the end. Every example below does both checks.

## Example 1: An increase round

*Rnd 5: \*Sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st; rep from \* around. (30 sts)* The round below has 24 stitches.

- **In plain words:** three plain sc, then an increase, over and over.
- **Each repeat** uses 4 stitches of the round below and makes 5.
- **Repeats:** 24 ÷ 4 = 6. **Made:** 6 × 5 = 30 ✓

## Example 2: A decrease round

*Rnd 12: \*Sc in next 2 sts, sc2tog; rep from \* around. (18 sts)* The round below has 24.

- **Each repeat** uses 4 stitches (2 plain + 2 worked together) and makes 3.
- **Repeats:** 24 ÷ 4 = 6. **Made:** 6 × 3 = 18 ✓

## Example 3: A foundation row with shells

*Ch 26. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, \*sk 2 ch, 5 dc in next ch, sk 2 ch, sc in next ch; rep from \* across. (4 shells, 5 sc)*

- **Chains used:** the 1 skipped ch, the first sc, then 6 chains per repeat.
- **Repeats:** (26 − 2) ÷ 6 = 4 shells, each followed by a sc, plus the first sc = 5 sc ✓

## Example 4: Brackets worked in one place

*(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 sp.*

- **In plain words:** everything inside the brackets goes into the same space: 3 dc, then 2 chains, then 3 more dc.
- **Result:** 6 dc and a new ch-2 space, which becomes a corner. Four of these make the corners of a granny round.

## Example 5: Multiple sizes

*Ch 61 (69, 77, 85). Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (60 (68, 76, 84) sts)* Gauge: 16 sc = 4 in.

- **Your size:** the first number is the smallest size; each number in brackets is the next size up. Highlight yours.
- **Stitches:** chain minus 1 (the skipped chain): 60, 68, 76, 84 ✓
- **Width:** at 4 sc per inch: 15, 17, 19 and 21 in.

## Example 6: "To last … sts"

*Row 2: Ch 1, sc in first 2 sts, \*FPdc around next st, sc in next 3 sts; rep from \* to last 3 sts, FPdc around next st, sc in last 2 sts.* Row 1 has 25 stitches.

- **Set-up:** 2 sc.
- **Repeats:** each uses 4 stitches. Stop when 3 remain: (25 − 2 − 3) ÷ 4 = 5 repeats.
- **Ending:** FPdc + 2 sc = 3 stitches.
- **Check:** 2 + 5 × 4 + 3 = 25 ✓, with 6 FPdc in the row. A row like this needs a multiple of 4 + 5 stitches.

## Example 7: Repeating a block of rows

*Rep Rows 2–3 five more times.*

- **In plain words:** you have already worked Rows 2–3 once; now work them 5 more times, 6 times in all.
- **Total rows:** Row 1 + 6 × 2 = 13 rows.

## Example 8: A turning chain that counts

*Row 4: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. Dc in next st and in each st across. (20 dc)* Row 3 has 20 stitches.

- **The ch-3** stands in for the first dc, so you skip the first stitch and work in the second.
- **Made:** 1 (the ch-3) + 19 dc = 20 ✓. The last dc goes into the top of the previous row's turning chain, if it counted as a stitch.

## Example 9: Work even

*Rnds 6–10: Sc in each st around. (30 sts)*

- **In plain words:** five rounds with no increases or decreases.
- **Rounds:** 10 − 6 + 1 = 5.

## Example 10: Evenly spaced decreases

*Next rnd: Decrease 7 sts evenly around. (53 sts)* The round below has 60.

- **Plan:** 60 ÷ 7 ≈ 8.6, so use 7 groups of 8 or 9 stitches, each ending in a decrease.
- **Arithmetic:** 3 groups of 8 + 4 groups of 9 = 60 stitches used. Each group makes one stitch fewer than it uses, so 60 − 7 = 53 ✓
- **Write it as:** \*(sc in next 7 sts, sc2tog), (sc in next 6 sts, sc2tog); rep from \* twice more (3 times in all), then sc in next 7 sts, sc2tog. Check: 4 groups of 9 (36 sts) + 3 groups of 8 (24 sts) = 60 stitches used.

## Example 11: Chart and text together

A chart shows Row 1 at the bottom with the row number on the right. That means Row 1 is read from right to left (for right-handed crochet). If the written text and the chart disagree, check the pattern's errata first. If there is none, compare both with the photo and with your own count.

## Project skill levels

Many patterns mark a skill level. The Craft Yarn Council describes four project levels (craftyarncouncil.com/standards/project-levels, checked November 2026):

| Level | What to expect |
|---|---|
| 1 · Basic | Basic stitches; may include basic increases and decreases |
| 2 · Easy | Simple stitch patterns, colorwork and/or shaping |
| 3 · Intermediate | More involved stitch patterns, colorwork and/or shaping |
| 4 · Complex | Complex stitch patterns, colorwork and/or shaping, using a variety of techniques at the same time |

The patterns in this book use plain-language labels. "Beginner" corresponds roughly to Level 1. "Advanced beginner" is between Levels 1 and 2. "Intermediate" is Levels 2–3.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Check a pattern's count, not just your own
> Patterns can contain errors. If your count is right by the arithmetic above and the printed count disagrees, the pattern may be wrong. Look for errata, or ask the designer.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Read it, count it, then crochet it. In that order.
