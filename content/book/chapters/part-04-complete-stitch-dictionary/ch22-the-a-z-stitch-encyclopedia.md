This encyclopedia gathers the stitches you will meet in patterns, from the chain to colorwork methods, in one consistent format. Each entry gives the US name and abbreviation, the UK equivalent, the approximate height, how to work it in US terms, what it is good for, and one practical tip. The chart symbol shown is the one most commonly used; always check the key printed with your pattern, because designers sometimes define stitches differently.

> [!STATUS]
> Entries are standard constructions written for this edition and checked against the loop-count rules in Chapter 18. Where designers disagree about a definition (extended stitches, clusters, lemon peel, bean and star stitches), the entry says so. No new swatches were made for this edition.

**How the entries are laid out.** 222 entries. The 44 stitches you will use most have large close-up step diagrams, two to a row, like the photo how-tos. The other full entries have a smaller step strip. Shorter cards (variations, joins, edgings and named stitch patterns) give the written method, its use and a tip, with no diagram yet.

## Foundation stitches

### Chain

![ch symbol](../../assets/symbols/ch.svg)

**US:** ch · **UK:** chain (ch) · **Height:** Foundation unit; very little height

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** With a loop on the hook, yarn over and draw the yarn through the loop on the hook.

### Foundation half double crochet

![T symbol](../../assets/symbols/T.svg)

**US:** fhdc · **UK:** foundation half treble (fhtr) · **Height:** A chain base plus hdc

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** Ch 3, yarn over, insert in 3rd ch from hook, yarn over, draw up a loop (3 loops), yarn over and draw through 1 (this is the 'chain'), yarn over and draw through all 3. For each next stitch: yarn over, insert under the two loops of the 'chain' of the previous stitch, yarn over, draw up a loop, yarn over and draw through 1, yarn over and draw through all 3.

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_foundation_half_double_crochet.png)

**Use it for:** Stretchy first rows for hdc hats, cowls and blankets.

**Common mistake:** Working too tightly, so the first row will not stretch to match the fabric above it.

**Tip:** Count the tops, not the chain edge: each fhdc gives one stitch for the next row.

### Adjustable ring (magic ring)

![ring symbol](../../assets/symbols/ring.svg)

**US:** magic ring / MR · **UK:** magic ring · **Height:** Not a stitch; a closable center

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** Wrap the yarn into a loop with the tail crossing behind the working yarn. Insert the hook through the loop, draw up the working yarn, chain 1 to secure (this chain does not count), work the first round into the ring over both strands, then pull the tail to close the center.

**Use it for:** Closed centers for amigurumi, hats worked top-down, and motifs.

**Common mistake:** Weaving the tail in only one direction, so the ring opens in wear.

**Tip:** Weave the tail in two directions before you trust the ring; a single pass can work loose.

### Chain ring

![ch symbol](../../assets/symbols/ch.svg)

**US:** ch-ring · **UK:** chain ring · **Height:** Not a stitch; a fixed center

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** Chain the number given, then slip stitch into the first chain to form a ring. Work the first round into the center of the ring, not into the chains.

![Step 1: Chain the number given](../../assets/bigsteps/st_chain_ring-01.svg)

1. Chain the number given

![Step 2: Sl st into the first ch](../../assets/bigsteps/st_chain_ring-02.svg)

2. Sl st into the first ch

![Step 3: Work round 1 into the center](../../assets/bigsteps/st_chain_ring-03.svg)

3. Work round 1 into the center


**Use it for:** Motifs and lace that are meant to have a small open center.

**Common mistake:** Working too tightly, so the first row will not stretch to match the fabric above it.

**Tip:** If a pattern says 'ch 4, join with sl st to form a ring' and you prefer a magic ring, the center will be smaller; check that the first round still lies flat.

### Stacked single crochet (chainless start)

![x symbol](../../assets/symbols/x.svg)

**US:** stacked sc · **UK:** stacked dc · **Height:** About the height of a dc

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** Single crochet in the first stitch. Insert the hook under the left vertical leg of that sc, yarn over, draw up a loop, yarn over and draw through both loops. The pair stands in for the first dc.

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_stacked_single_crochet_chainless_start.png)

**Use it for:** Straighter row edges in dc fabric than a ch-3 turning chain gives.

**Common mistake:** Working too tightly, so the first row will not stretch to match the fabric above it.

**Tip:** The stacked pair counts as the first dc of the row; work the last dc of the next row into its top.

### Foundation treble crochet

![T2 symbol](../../assets/symbols/T2.svg)

**US:** ftr · **UK:** foundation double treble (fdtr) · **Height:** A chain base plus tr

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Multiple:** Any number

**How to work it:** Ch 4, yarn over twice, insert in 4th ch from hook, draw up a loop, yarn over and draw through 1 (the 'chain'), then (yarn over and draw through 2) three times. For each next stitch, yarn over twice and insert in the 'chain' of the previous stitch.

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_foundation_treble_crochet.png)

**Use it for:** Stretchy starts for tall lace and treble fabrics.

**Common mistake:** Working too tightly, so the first row will not stretch to match the fabric above it.

**Tip:** Hold the growing foundation with your thumb on the 'chain' so you can find it.

#### Standing half double crochet

**US:** standing hdc · **UK:** standing htr · **Height:** About 2 chains tall · **Difficulty:** Beginner

**How to work it:** Make a slip knot on the hook and hold it with a finger. Then work the stitch as usual into the stitch or space where the new yarn starts: yarn over, insert into the stitch or space, yarn over and draw up a loop (3 loops), yarn over and draw through all 3.

**Use it for:** Joining a new color with a real stitch instead of a slip stitch and chains. **Tip:** Weave the slip-knot tail in the opposite direction from the working yarn.

#### Standing treble crochet

**US:** standing tr · **UK:** standing dtr · **Height:** About 4 chains tall · **Difficulty:** Beginner

**How to work it:** Make a slip knot on the hook and hold it with a finger. Then work the stitch as usual into the stitch or space where the new yarn starts: yarn over 2 times, insert into the stitch or space, yarn over and draw up a loop (4 loops), [yarn over and draw through 2] 2 times.

**Use it for:** Joining a new color with a real stitch instead of a slip stitch and chains. **Tip:** Weave the slip-knot tail in the opposite direction from the working yarn.

#### Magic ring with double crochet

**US:** dc in magic ring · **UK:** tr in magic ring · **Height:** About 3 chains tall · **Difficulty:** Beginner

**How to work it:** Make an adjustable ring, ch 3 (counts as dc, or ch 2 if the pattern says it does not count), work the stated number of dc into the ring, pull the tail to close, and join to the first stitch.

**Use it for:** Flat circles, flowers and granny squares worked from the center. **Tip:** Pull the tail firmly, then pull again after the first round; a dc ring loosens more than a sc ring.

### Invisible join

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Multiple:** Set by the edging

**How to work it:** After the last stitch of the round, cut the yarn and pull the tail through the loop. Thread it on a tapestry needle, pass it under both loops of the first true stitch of the round, then back down through the center of the last stitch.

**Use it for:** Finishing a round with no visible bump, especially on borders and motifs.

**Common mistake:** Working too many or too few stitches along row ends, so the edge ruffles or pulls in.

**Tip:** The join itself makes a fake V; pull it to the same tension as the stitches around it.

#### Single crochet edging

**US:** sc edging · **UK:** dc edging · **Height:** About 1 chain tall · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** Work sc evenly around the edge: 1 sc in each stitch along the top and bottom, about 1 sc per sc row end and 2 sc per dc row end along the sides, and 3 sc in each corner.

**Use it for:** Neatening the edges of blankets, squares and garments before a fancy border. **Tip:** If the edge ruffles, use fewer stitches; if it pulls in, use more.

#### Shell edging

**US:** shell edge · **UK:** shell edge · **Height:** About 3 chains tall · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** Over a multiple of 6 + 1: sc in the first st, *sk 2 sts, 5 dc in the next st, sk 2 sts, sc in the next st; rep from * across.

**Use it for:** Classic scalloped borders for blankets and baby clothes. **Tip:** Count the edge before starting so the repeat ends with a sc in the last stitch.

#### Chain-loop edging

**US:** ch-3 loop edge · **UK:** ch-3 loop edge · **Height:** Small loops · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** *Sc in the next st, ch 3, sk 1 st; rep from * across.

**Use it for:** Light, lacy borders and ties for drawstrings. **Tip:** Vary the chain length (ch 3, ch 5) for bigger loops.

#### Picot edging with chain 3

**US:** picot edge (sc, picot) · **UK:** picot edge · **Height:** Small points · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** *Sc in each of the next 3 sts, ch 3, sl st in the 3rd ch from the hook; rep from * across.

**Use it for:** Pretty edges on baby clothes, doilies and collars. **Tip:** Work the picots looser than you think; tight picots disappear.

#### Bobble edging

**US:** bobble edge · **UK:** bobble edge · **Height:** Raised points · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** *Sc in each of the next 3 sts, 4-dc bobble in the next st; rep from * across.

**Use it for:** Playful edges for blankets and pillows. **Tip:** Bobbles pop to the back of the row you work them on; check which side shows.

#### Corded edge with reverse half double crochet

**US:** reverse hdc · **UK:** reverse htr · **Height:** About 2 chains tall · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** Working from left to right (right-handed), yarn over, insert into the next stitch to the right, yarn over and draw up a loop, yarn over and draw through all 3 loops.

**Use it for:** A thicker twisted edge than reverse single crochet. **Tip:** Work slowly; the backward motion is awkward at first.

#### Chain-two space corner (sc borders)

**US:** (sc, ch 2, sc) in corner · **UK:** (dc, ch 2, dc) · **Height:** About 1 chain tall · **Difficulty:** Easy

**How to work it:** Work (sc, ch 2, sc) in each corner stitch or space instead of 3 sc.

**Use it for:** Crisp, square corners on sc borders. **Tip:** A ch-2 corner is flatter than 3 sc in very thick yarns.

#### Single crochet seam

**US:** sc seam · **UK:** dc seam · **Height:** Raised ridge · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Hold two pieces together and work sc through both layers, one stitch or row end at a time. With wrong sides together the ridge shows on the right side; with right sides together it is hidden inside.

**Use it for:** Joining granny squares, panels and bags with a sturdy seam. **Tip:** Match stitch for stitch; if one edge is longer, skip a stitch on it evenly.

#### Slip stitch seam

**US:** sl st seam · **UK:** ss seam · **Height:** Flat ridge · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Hold the two pieces together and work slip stitches through both layers, inserting under the matching loops of each edge.

**Use it for:** Neat, low seams in garments and blankets. **Tip:** Keep the slip stitches loose so the seam stretches with the fabric.

#### Whip stitch seam

**US:** whip stitch · **UK:** whip stitch · **Height:** Flat · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** With a tapestry needle and matching yarn, pass the needle through both edges from the same side, one stitch at a time, working in a spiral over the edges.

**Use it for:** Joining squares and amigurumi parts with a nearly flat seam. **Tip:** Work through the same loops (both, or back loops only) on both pieces.

#### Mattress stitch seam

**US:** mattress stitch · **UK:** mattress stitch · **Height:** Invisible from the right side · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Lay the pieces side by side, right sides up. With a tapestry needle, pass under one stitch or row end on one side, then the matching one on the other side, zigzagging; pull gently every few stitches so the seam closes.

**Use it for:** Invisible seams on garments and sideways pieces. **Tip:** Do not pull so tight that the seam puckers.

#### Flat slip stitch join

**US:** flat sl st join (zigzag) · **UK:** flat ss join · **Height:** Very flat · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Lay two squares side by side, right sides up. Slip stitch into a loop on one edge, then into the matching loop on the other, alternating from side to side.

**Use it for:** Flat, lacy joins for granny-square blankets. **Tip:** Work into the back loops only for the flattest result.

#### Join-as-you-go

**US:** JAYG · **UK:** JAYG · **Height:** As the last round · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Work the last round of a motif, and at each joining point replace a chain with a slip stitch (or a sc) into the matching chain space of the neighboring finished motif, then continue the round.

**Use it for:** Joining granny squares and hexagons without sewing. **Tip:** Plan the order in which motifs are joined before you start; corners with four motifs need extra care.

#### Zipper join

**US:** zipper join (sl st through outer loops) · **UK:** zipper join · **Height:** Small ridge · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** With the right sides facing up, slip stitch through the outer loops of the two edges, alternating pieces, to make a ridge like a zipper.

**Use it for:** Decorative joins in contrasting colors. **Tip:** Use a contrast color to show the ridge, or a matching one to hide it.

## Colorwork stitches

### Overlay mosaic

![T1 symbol](../../assets/symbols/T1.svg)

**US:** overlay mosaic · **UK:** overlay mosaic · **Height:** sc base, dc spikes

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Rows are worked in sc (often BLO), with dc worked in front of the previous rows into a lower row to place color; each row uses one color.

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_overlay_mosaic.png)

**Use it for:** Geometric colorwork without carried yarn.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Most overlay mosaic patterns cut the yarn at the end of every row, leaving fringe.

### Inset mosaic

![ch symbol](../../assets/symbols/ch.svg)

**US:** inset mosaic · **UK:** inset mosaic · **Height:** sc base with chain spaces

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Rows combine sc and chain-spaces; dc worked into chain-spaces of lower rows (from the right side) fill in the color pattern.

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_inset_mosaic.png)

**Use it for:** Reversible-looking geometric colorwork.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** The chart key defines which symbols mean ch-sp and which mean dropped dc—check it.

### Larksfoot stitch

![spike symbol](../../assets/symbols/spike.svg)

**US:** larksfoot · **UK:** larksfoot · **Height:** sc base with long stitches

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Rows alternate sc and ch-1 spaces; on the next row, a long stitch is worked over each ch-1 into the skipped stitch of the row below, usually changing color every two rows.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p05-granny-stack.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_larksfoot_stitch.png)

**Use it for:** Two-color textured blankets without carried yarn.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Definitions vary (some use a long dc, others a long sc); follow one pattern's rows.

### Tapestry single crochet

![x symbol](../../assets/symbols/x.svg)

**US:** tapestry sc · **UK:** tapestry dc · **Height:** As sc

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Work sc over the color not in use, so it is carried inside the stitches. Change color by drawing the new color through the last two loops of the stitch before the new color begins.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p05-granny-wip.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_tapestry_single_crochet.png)

**Use it for:** Charted pictures, bags and baskets.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** See the Tapestry Crochet Masterclass for ways to keep the motif from slanting in rounds.

### Intarsia crochet

![x symbol](../../assets/symbols/x.svg)

**US:** intarsia · **UK:** intarsia · **Height:** Any stitch

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Each color area has its own ball or bobbin. Work to the color change, change color on the last yarn over of the stitch before, drop the old color on the wrong side and leave it for the next row.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p05-hexagons.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_intarsia_crochet.png)

**Use it for:** Large color areas, letters and pictures without carried strands.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Wind small bobbins and let them hang free; untwist them at the end of each row.

### Planned pooling

![x symbol](../../assets/symbols/x.svg)

**US:** planned pooling · **UK:** planned pooling · **Height:** Any stitch, usually moss stitch

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** With a yarn that repeats its colors at regular lengths, adjust stitch count and tension so each color lands on the same place every row, making an argyle or column pattern.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p03-pattern-granny.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_planned_pooling.png)

**Use it for:** Striking argyle patterns from variegated yarn.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Works only with yarns that have a regular, short color repeat; you may need to change hook size by a fraction and loosen or tighten single stitches.

### Interlocking crochet

![T1 symbol](../../assets/symbols/T1.svg)

**US:** interlocking crochet · **UK:** interlocking crochet · **Height:** Two mesh layers

**Difficulty:** Experienced · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** Two colors of filet mesh are worked alternately so that the dc of each layer pass through the spaces of the other, locking them together into one reversible, two-faced fabric.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p00-coasters.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_interlocking_crochet.png)

**Use it for:** Reversible geometric pictures, cowls and blankets.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Interlocking charts show both layers in one grid; read the key for which color forms the front squares before you start.

### Stripes carried up the side

![x symbol](../../assets/symbols/x.svg)

**US:** carry color up side · **UK:** carry colour up side · **Height:** Any stitch

**Difficulty:** Intermediate · **Multiple:** Set by the chart

**How to work it:** When stripes are an even number of rows, leave the unused color at the edge and pick it up when you need it, twisting it with the working yarn every couple of rows.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p00-cozy-corner.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_stripes_carried_up_the_side.png)

**Use it for:** Striped blankets and scarves without weaving dozens of ends.

**Common mistake:** Changing color too late: the new color must be pulled through on the last yarn over of the stitch before it.

**Tip:** Keep the carried strand loose enough that the edge does not pucker; check by stretching it gently.

#### Stranded colorwork (Fair Isle style)

**US:** stranded · **UK:** stranded · **Height:** As the stitch · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Work with two colors in the same row. Carry the color not in use loosely along the back of the work and change colors on the last yarn over of the stitch before each new color.

**Use it for:** Patterned hats and mittens with floats on the back. **Tip:** Keep floats short and loose; long floats snag. Tapestry crochet (carrying the strand inside the stitches) avoids floats.

#### Cross-stitch on crochet

**US:** cross stitch · **UK:** cross stitch · **Height:** Embroidery · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Using a tapestry needle and contrast yarn, embroider X shapes over the squares made by sc stitches, following a cross-stitch chart.

**Use it for:** Adding small motifs or letters after the fabric is finished. **Tip:** Single crochet fabric worked in the back loops gives a neater grid.

#### Color change at the end of a row

**US:** row-end color change · **UK:** row-end color change · **Height:** As the row · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Work the last stitch of the row until the final yarn over, drop the old color, and complete the stitch with the new color. Chain and turn with the new color.

**Use it for:** Stripes without a stray loop of the old color at the edge. **Tip:** Leave 6 in (15 cm) tails of both colors for weaving in.

#### Surface crochet in back loops

**US:** surface crochet · **UK:** surface crochet · **Height:** On top of fabric · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**How to work it:** Hold the yarn behind the fabric. Insert the hook through the fabric, draw up a loop, then insert again a short distance away and draw the next loop through the loop on the hook, making a chain on the surface.

**Use it for:** Adding lines, plaids and outlines after the fabric is made. **Tip:** Work loosely so the fabric does not pucker.
