A stitch pattern is a small set of rows that repeats to make fabric. Each entry below gives the chain multiple, the rows in US terms, a note on how the counts work, and a schematic chart. Charts show the order and type of stitches; they are not drawn to scale.

> [!STATUS]
> These are traditional stitch patterns written out for this edition. The chain formulas and edge counts were derived by hand and rechecked; **no swatches were made for this edition.** Work a swatch of at least two repeats before starting a project.

## Moss Stitch (Linen Stitch)

**Multiple:** Ch an even number · **Good for:** Scarves, dishcloths, planned pooling

1. Row 1: Sc in 4th ch from hook, *ch 1, sk next ch, sc in next ch; rep from * across.
2. Row 2: Ch 2, turn. Sc in first ch-1 sp, *ch 1, sk next sc, sc in next ch-1 sp; rep from * across, working the last sc into the space made by the skipped chains (or the turning ch-2 on later rows).
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_moss (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_moss.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Moss stitch (linen / granite stitch)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_moss_stitch_linen_granite_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Each sc goes into a space, never into a stitch. The count of sc stays the same every row.

## Griddle Stitch

**Multiple:** Ch an odd number · **Good for:** Washcloths, dense blankets

1. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, *dc in next ch, sc in next ch; rep from * to last ch, dc in last ch.
2. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first st (a dc), *dc in next st (a sc), sc in next st (a dc); rep from * to last st, dc in last st.
3. Rep Row 2.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p04-bobble-blanket.jpg)

![sl_griddle (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_griddle.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Griddle stitch

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_griddle_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Sc always goes into a dc and dc into a sc, which makes the bumpy texture. Stitch count is even.

## Suzette Stitch

**Multiple:** Ch an even number · **Good for:** Drapey blankets, bags

1. Row 1: (Sc, dc) in 2nd ch from hook, *sk next ch, (sc, dc) in next ch; rep from * to last 2 ch, sk next ch, sc in last ch.
2. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. (Sc, dc) in first sc, *sk next dc, (sc, dc) in next sc; rep from * to last 2 sts, sk next dc, sc in last sc.
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_suzette (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_suzette.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Suzette stitch

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_suzette_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Each pair sits in the sc of the pair below. Every row ends with a single sc.

## V-Stitch Rows

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 3 + 1 (at least 10) · **Good for:** Light shawls, summer tops

1. Row 1: (Dc, ch 1, dc) in 5th ch from hook (V-st made), *sk 2 ch, V-st in next ch; rep from * to last 2 ch, sk next ch, dc in last ch.
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. V-st in the ch-1 sp of each V-st across, dc in the top of the turning chain (on Row 2, in the top of the skipped chains at the edge).
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_vstitch (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_vstitch.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** V-stitch

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_v_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> V-stitches stack into the spaces below; only the edge stitches are worked into stitches.

## Classic Shell Stitch

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 6 + 2 · **Good for:** Blankets, edgings, baby items

1. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, *sk 2 ch, 5 dc in next ch, sk 2 ch, sc in next ch; rep from * across.
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. 2 dc in first sc, *sk 2 dc, sc in next dc (the center of the shell), sk 2 dc, 5 dc in next sc; rep from *, ending the last repeat with 3 dc in the last sc instead of 5.
3. Row 3: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first dc, *sk 2 dc, 5 dc in next sc, sk 2 dc, sc in next dc (center); rep from *, working the last sc in the top of the turning chain.
4. Rep Rows 2–3.

![sl_shell (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_shell.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Shell

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p04-swatch-stack.jpg)

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_shell.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Row 2 starts and ends with half shells, so the edges stay straight.

## Crossed Double Crochet

**Multiple:** Ch an even number · **Good for:** Woven-look scarves and bags

1. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook (skipped ch count as dc) and in each ch across.
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. *Sk next st, dc in next st, dc in the skipped st working in front of the dc just made; rep from * to last st, dc in top of turning chain.
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_crossed (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_crossed.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Crossed double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_crossed_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> The stitch count stays the same; the crossing pairs sit over the pairs below.

## Granny Stripe

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 3 + 1 (at least 10) · **Good for:** Stash-busting blankets

1. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook (skipped ch count as dc), *sk 2 ch, 3 dc in next ch; rep from * to last 3 ch, sk 2 ch, 2 dc in last ch.
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. 3 dc in each sp between groups across, dc in top of turning chain.
3. Row 3: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. Dc in the first sp (between the edge dc and the first group), *3 dc in next sp between groups; rep from * to the last sp, dc in last sp, dc in top of turning chain.
4. Rep Rows 2–3, changing color as you like.

![sl_grannystripe (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_grannystripe.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Granny cluster

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_granny_cluster.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Groups always go into the spaces between groups. Row 2 has one more group than Row 1; Row 3 returns to half-groups at the edges.

## Scattered Bobble Fabric

**Multiple:** Ch an even number · **Good for:** Pillows, baby blankets

1. Bobble: [yo, insert in st, draw up a loop, yo, draw through 2] 5 times in the same st, yo, draw through all 6 loops.
2. Row 1 (RS): Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (Odd number of sts.)
3. Row 2 (WS): Ch 1, turn. Sc in first st, *bobble in next st, sc in next st; rep from * across.
4. Row 3: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across.
5. Row 4: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first 2 sts, *bobble in next st, sc in next st; rep from * to last st, sc in last st.
6. Row 5: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across.
7. Rep Rows 2–5.

![sl_bobble (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_bobble.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Bobble

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_bobble.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Bobbles are worked on WS rows so they pop to the RS. The top of each bobble counts as one stitch.

## Basketweave

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 8 + 4 · **Good for:** Thick blankets, pillows

1. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook (skipped ch count as dc) and in each ch across.
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. *FPdc around next 4 sts, BPdc around next 4 sts; rep from * to last st, dc in top of turning chain.
3. Rows 3–4: Ch 3, turn. Work FPdc around each post that is raised toward you and BPdc around each post that is recessed, dc in top of turning chain.
4. Row 5: Ch 3, turn. Work BPdc around each raised post and FPdc around each recessed post (this switches the blocks), dc in top of turning chain.
5. Rows 6–7: As Rows 3–4.
6. Rep Rows 5–7.

![sl_basket (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_basket.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Front post double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_front_post_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> ‘Raised’ and ‘recessed’ mean as the stitches face you on the row you are working.

## Spike-Stitch Stripes

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 4 + 2 · **Good for:** Two-color blankets and cushions without carried yarn

1. Rows 1–4 (color A): Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. Rows 2–4: ch 1, turn, sc in each st across.
2. Row 5 (color B, join at the edge): Ch 1, turn. Sc in first st, *long sc in the next st 2 rows below (draw the loop up to the working height), sc in next 3 sts; rep from * across.
3. Rows 6–8 (same color as Row 5): Ch 1, turn, sc in each st across.
4. Row 9 (change to the other color): As Row 5.
5. Rep Rows 6–9: four rows in each color, with every spike row worked in the new color.

![sl_spike (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_spike.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Spike stitch

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_spike_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> The stitch count is a multiple of 4 + 1 throughout. Each long sc replaces a sc in the current row; it does not add a stitch.

## Sedge Stitch

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 3 · **Good for:** Dense dishcloths and blankets

1. Row 1: (Hdc, dc) in 3rd ch from hook (the skipped 2 ch count as a sc), *sk 2 ch, (sc, hdc, dc) in next ch; rep from * to last 3 ch, sk 2 ch, sc in last ch.
2. Row 2: Ch 1 (counts as sc), turn. (Hdc, dc) in first sc, *sk next 2 sts, (sc, hdc, dc) in next sc; rep from * to last 3 sts, sk 2 sts, sc in top of turning chain.
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_sedge (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_sedge.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Sedge stitch

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_sedge_stitch.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Every group sits in the sc of a group below, and each row ends with one sc in the turning chain, so the count never changes.

## Single Crochet Back-Loop Rib

**Multiple:** Any number of chains (the band width) + 1 · **Good for:** Hat bands, cuffs, ear warmers (worked sideways)

1. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across.
2. Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc in the back loop only of each st across.
3. Rep Row 2 until the band is long enough, then seam the short ends.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p05-blanket-chair.jpg)

![sl_blorib (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_blorib.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Back loop only

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_back_loop_only.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> The ridges run along the rows; turned sideways, the band stretches around the head or wrist.

## Herringbone Half Double

**Multiple:** Any number of chains + 2 · **Good for:** Woven-look cowls and scarves

1. Herringbone hdc (hhdc): yo, insert in st, draw up a loop and draw it straight through the first loop on the hook, yo, draw through both loops.
2. Row 1: Hhdc in 3rd ch from hook (skipped ch do not count) and in each ch across.
3. Row 2: Ch 2 (does not count), turn. Hhdc in each st across.
4. Rep Row 2.

![sl_herring (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_herring.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Herringbone half double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_herringbone_half_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Each row slants; because the work turns, the slant alternates and forms a herringbone.

## Post-Stitch Ribbing (worked in rows)

**Multiple:** Ch an even number (at least 10) · **Good for:** Cuffs, brims and sweater hems worked flat

1. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook (the skipped 3 ch count as a dc) and in each ch across. (An even number of sts.)
2. Row 2: Ch 2 (counts as an edge st), turn. *FPdc around the next st, BPdc around the next st; rep from * to the last st, hdc in the top of the turning ch.
3. Row 3: Ch 2, turn. Work FPdc around each stitch whose post leans toward you and BPdc around each stitch whose post sinks away from you, to the last st; hdc in the top of the turning ch.
4. Rep Row 3.

![sl_postrib (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_postrib.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Back post double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_back_post_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Chain N gives N − 2 stitches in Row 1. The edge stitches take 2, so the ribbing needs an even number in the middle: N must be even. The Row 3 rule keeps each column raised on the same face of the fabric.

## Third-Loop Half Double (Camel Stitch)

**Multiple:** Any number of chains + 2 · **Good for:** Knit-look ribbed hats, cowls and blanket borders

1. Row 1: Hdc in 3rd ch from hook (the skipped ch do not count) and in each ch across.
2. Row 2: Ch 2 (does not count), turn. Hdc in the third loop (the horizontal bar below the top V, on the side facing you) of each st across.
3. Rep Row 2.

![sl_camel (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_camel.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Third-loop half double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_third_loop_half_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Each row has the same number of stitches as Row 1 (chains − 2). Because the hdc goes into the bar behind the V, the top V rolls forward and forms a ridge.

## Waffle Stitch

**Multiple:** Ch a multiple of 3 + 2 (at least 11) · **Good for:** Thick dishcloths, blankets and cushion covers

1. Row 1: Dc in 4th ch from hook (the skipped 3 ch count as a dc) and in each ch across. (A multiple of 3 sts.)
2. Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. *FPdc around the next st, dc in each of the next 2 sts; rep from * to the last 2 sts, FPdc around the next st, dc in the top of the turning ch.
3. Row 3: Ch 3 (counts as dc), turn. *Dc in the next FPdc, FPdc around each of the next 2 dc; rep from * to the last 2 sts, dc in the next FPdc, dc in the top of the turning ch.
4. Rep Rows 2 and 3.

![sl_waffle (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/sl_waffle.png)

**Key stitch, step by step:** Front post double crochet

![steps (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/st_front_post_double_crochet.png)

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Chain 3k + 5 gives 3k + 3 stitches. Each row uses 1 edge stitch, k repeats of 3, then 2 stitches at the end: 1 + 3k + 2 = 3k + 3, so the count never changes. The raised posts form a grid of little squares.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Swatch two repeats, not one
> One repeat tells you what a stitch looks like. Two repeats, and at least four rows, tell you how it behaves at the edges and whether your counts hold.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — The multiple is for the chain or the stitches; check which
> Some patterns give the multiple for the foundation chain, others for the stitches in Row 1. The two differ by the turning chain. This library always gives the chain.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Make a little square of every stitch pattern you try and stitch them into a sampler blanket. You will have a reference and a gift at once.


> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Joining color matters
> The joining color changes how a granny blanket looks more than any single square. Test two or three joining colors on four squares before deciding.

> [!HINT] Corner pins for squares
> Pin each corner of a square first when blocking, then the middles of the sides.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Seams stretch in the wash
> Seams worked too loosely can stretch over time. Join with a firm, even tension and the same fiber as the squares.
