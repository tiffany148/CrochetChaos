Motifs are the building blocks of granny blankets, bags and garments. This chapter gives five classic motifs written round by round, with stitch-count tables computed for rounds 1–12 so you can stop at any size. Placement and joining are covered in Chapters 28 and 24.

> [!STATUS]
> These are traditional motif constructions written out for this edition, with counts derived from the round structure and checked by computer. No samples were made for this edition. Different yarns lie flatter or ruffle at different rates; if a motif cups or waves, see the notes after each one.

## Solid granny square

> [!PATTERN] Solid Granny Square
> Rnd 1: Make a magic ring (or ch 4 and join with sl st). Ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in ring, ch 2, *3 dc in ring, ch 2; rep from * 2 more times. Join with sl st to top of ch-3. (12 dc, 4 ch-2 sps)
> Rnd 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc, worked in the same st as the join), dc in next 2 dc, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in next ch-2 sp, dc in next 3 dc; rep from * 2 more times, (2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in last ch-2 sp. Join. (28 dc: 7 per side)
> Rnd 3 and on: Ch 3 (counts as dc), dc in each dc to the next corner, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in the corner sp, dc in each dc to the next corner; rep from * around. Join. Each round adds 4 dc to each side.

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Side n has 4n − 1 dc (counting the corner dc on each side of the ch-2), so round n has 16n − 4 dc in all.

## Classic granny square (summary)

The full pattern is in Chapter 28. Round n has 4n clusters of 3 dc (12n dc), 4 corner ch-2 spaces and 4(n − 1) side ch-1 spaces.

## Granny hexagon

> [!PATTERN] Granny Hexagon
> Rnd 1: Make a magic ring (or ch 4 and join). Ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in ring, ch 2, *3 dc in ring, ch 2; rep from * 4 more times. Join to top of ch-3. (6 clusters, 6 ch-2 sps)
> Rnd 2: Sl st in next 2 dc and into the ch-2 sp. Ch 3 (counts as dc), (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in same sp, ch 1, *(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 sp, ch 1; rep from * 4 more times. Join. (12 clusters)
> Rnd 3 and on: Sl st to the first corner sp and start as Rnd 2. Work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in each corner sp and 3 dc in each ch-1 side sp, with ch 1 between all groups. Join.

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Round n has 6n clusters (18n dc), 6 corner spaces and 6(n − 1) side spaces. If your hexagon ruffles, try ch 1 instead of ch 2 at the corners; if it cups, try ch 2 or ch 3.

## Granny triangle

> [!PATTERN] Granny Triangle
> Rnd 1: Magic ring. Ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in ring, ch 3, *3 dc in ring, ch 3; rep from * once more. Join. (3 clusters, 3 ch-3 sps)
> Rnd 2: Sl st in next 2 dc and into the ch-3 sp. Ch 3, (2 dc, ch 3, 3 dc) in same sp, ch 1, *(3 dc, ch 3, 3 dc) in next ch-3 sp, ch 1; rep from * once more. Join. (6 clusters)
> Rnd 3 and on: As Rnd 2, working 3 dc in each ch-1 side sp, with ch 1 between all groups.

> [!HINT] How the count works
> Round n has 3n clusters (9n dc), 3 corner spaces and 3(n − 1) side spaces. Triangles need sharper corners than squares, which is why the corner chain is longer; adjust it if your triangle cups or ruffles.

## Flat double-crochet circle

> [!PATTERN] Flat Circle Motif
> Rnd 1: Magic ring. Ch 3 (counts as dc), 11 dc in ring. Join. (12 dc)
> Rnd 2: Ch 3 (counts as dc), dc in same st, 2 dc in each st around. Join. (24 dc)
> Rnd 3: Ch 3, 2 dc in next st, *dc in next st, 2 dc in next st; rep from * around. Join. (36 dc)
> Rnd n (n ≥ 3): Ch 3, then work the repeat *dc in next n − 2 sts, 2 dc in next st* 12 times in all, counting the ch-3 as the first dc. Join. (12n dc)

> [!HINT] Make a circle into a square
> On the final round of a circle with 48 or more stitches, work taller stitches (tr) in four evenly spaced places with chains between them to make corners, and shorter stitches (hdc or sc) between. Test the corner positions by folding the circle into quarters first.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Measure your motif after round 3
> Measure a motif after three rounds and again after five. The difference tells you how much each round adds, so you can predict how many rounds give the size you need.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Hexagons need half-hexagons at the edges
> A blanket of hexagons has jagged sides. Fill them with half-hexagon motifs, or finish with a border that builds up the low points.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> One color for the last round of every motif ties a scrappy blanket together like a frame around a picture.


> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Test steam on a swatch
> Steam can flatten texture and relax acrylic permanently. Always test on a swatch first.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> A mistake you know about is a mistake you can choose to keep. That is called a design feature.
