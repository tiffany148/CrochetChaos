This guide walks through a first granny-square blanket from start to finish. It joins up methods from across the book: yarn choice, gauge, the classic square, layout, joining and border. Each step names the chapter with the details.

> [!STATUS]
> This is a planning guide, not a tested pattern. The numbers in the examples are arithmetic. Measure your own blocked squares at each checkpoint.

## Step 1: Decide the size

Choose the finished size from the use: a lap blanket, a throw, or a bed size. The Ready-Reckoner (Chapter 67) turns a size and a square size into a number of squares. Leave room for the border: a border of three or four rounds can add 1–2 in on each side, depending on your stitches. Measure your own border on a test square.

## Step 2: Choose yarn and hook

- Worsted (CYC 4) acrylic or a washable blend is a forgiving first choice.
- Choose 3–6 colors plus one joining or border color. A neutral joining color calms a scrappy layout.
- Use the hook suggested on the label, then check your square (Step 3).

## Step 3: Make and measure a test square

Make one classic granny square (Chapter 28) to the round count you think you want. Block it, then measure it.

> **Example (arithmetic only):** a five-round square blocks to 6 in. For a 48 × 60 in throw before the border: 48 ÷ 6 = 8 squares across and 60 ÷ 6 = 10 squares down, which is 80 squares.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Measure the test square after blocking
> An unblocked granny square can measure noticeably smaller and be less square than a blocked one. Base your plan on the blocked size.

## Step 4: Plan the layout and colors

Choose a layout system: checkerboard, diagonal, no-neighbor, frames, stripes or planned scrappy (Chapter 28). Count how many squares you need in each colorway. The layout figures in that chapter show the counts for each system.

## Step 5: Estimate yarn

Weigh your blocked test square. Multiply by the number of squares, add the border estimate, and add a margin (Chapter 68).

> **Example (arithmetic only):** at 12 g per square, 80 squares = 960 g. A border swatch suggests about 150 g more. Total 1,110 g, plus a 10% margin ≈ 1,220 g, which is 13 balls of 100 g.

## Step 6: Make the squares

- Weave in the ends of each square as you finish it, or crochet over them as you go.
- Count corner spaces every round.
- Stack finished squares by colorway, with a tag for each pile.

## Step 7: Block

Block all the squares to the same size before joining (Chapter 63). Pin them to a grid mat or a measured board.

## Step 8: Join

Lay the squares out and photograph the layout. Join them in the order planned in Chapter 28: rows first, then columns, or join-as-you-go (Chapter 24).

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Check the photo every row
> Before joining each row, compare it with your layout photo. A single rotated square is easy to fix now and painful to fix later.

## Step 9: Border

Work a round of sc or dc evenly around the whole blanket, with corner groups in each corner. Add as many rounds as you like. Finish with a firm last round, such as sc or crab stitch (Chapter 26).

## Step 10: Finish and care

Weave in the remaining ends. Give the whole blanket a final light block. Attach a care card if it is a gift (Chapter 65).

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Eighty squares is just one square, eighty evenings. Put the finished ones in a basket where you can see them grow.
