An edging is the frame around your work. Most edgings are worked over a first round of plain single crochet, which evens out the edge and gives you a stitch count you can adjust to fit the repeat. Each edging below gives its multiple (the number of stitches along a side that the repeat needs), the instructions in US terms, a use, and a schematic chart.

> [!STATUS]
> Traditional edgings written out for this edition. Counts were derived by hand; no samples were made for this edition. Charts show the base round (bottom) and the edging (top).

> [!HINT] Fitting a repeat to a side
> Count the base sc between corners on each side. If an edging needs a multiple of 4 + 1 and a side has 87 sc, you need 85 or 89: add or skip one or two stitches evenly along that side on the base round. Corners are worked as 3 sc in the base round, and the edging usually treats each corner sc as its own repeat start.

## Plain single crochet border

**Multiple:** Any number · **Use:** The base for every other edging.

Sc in each st along the edge, 3 sc in each corner st; on row-end edges, work about 1 sc per sc row end, or 2 sc per dc row end, adjusting so the edge lies flat.

## Crab stitch (reverse sc)

**Multiple:** Any number, worked over a sc round · **Use:** A firm, corded edge for blankets and cuffs.

Working in the opposite direction to usual, *insert in the next st to the right, draw up a loop, yo, draw through both loops; rep from *.

![edge_02 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_02.png)

## Picot edge

**Multiple:** Multiple of 3 · **Use:** Dainty finish for baby items and doilies.

*Sc in next 3 sts, ch 3, sl st in 3rd ch from hook (picot made); rep from * along the edge.

![edge_03 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_03.png)

## Small scallop

**Multiple:** Multiple of 4 + 1 · **Use:** Gentle waves for blankets and collars.

Sc in first st, *sk next st, 3 dc in next st, sk next st, sc in next st; rep from *.

![edge_04 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_04.png)

## Shell edge

**Multiple:** Multiple of 6 + 1 · **Use:** Classic blanket border.

Sc in first st, *sk 2 sts, 5 dc in next st, sk 2 sts, sc in next st; rep from *.

![edge_05 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_05.png)

## V-stitch edge

**Multiple:** Multiple of 4 + 1 · **Use:** Light, airy border for shawls.

Sc in first st, *sk next st, (dc, ch 1, dc) in next st, sk next st, sc in next st; rep from *.

![edge_06 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_06.png)

## Chain-loop lace edge

**Multiple:** Multiple of 4 + 1 · **Use:** Quick lacy finish; add a second round of (3 sc, picot, 3 sc) in each loop for more texture.

Sc in first st, *ch 5, sk next 3 sts, sc in next st; rep from *.

![edge_07 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_07.png)

## Gentle ruffle

**Multiple:** Any number · **Use:** Baby blankets, dresses, pillow edges.

2 dc in each st along the edge. For a fuller ruffle, use 3 dc in each st, or work a second round of 2 dc in each st.

![edge_08 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_08.png)

## Post-stitch rib band

**Multiple:** Even number, over a dc round · **Use:** Cuffs, brims, cardigan bands.

Ch 2 (does not count), *FPdc around next st, BPdc around next st; rep from *. Repeat the round for the band depth.

![edge_09 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_09.png)

## Slip-stitch cord

**Multiple:** Any number · **Use:** Firming a stretched edge with almost no added width.

Sl st in each st along the edge, keeping the loops slightly loose.

![edge_10 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_10.png)

## Bobble edge

**Multiple:** Multiple of 2 + 1, worked with the WS facing · **Use:** Playful border for kids' blankets.

Sc in first st, *3-dc bobble in next st, sc in next st; rep from *. Bobbles pop toward the side facing away from you, so work this round with the wrong side facing.

![edge_11 (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/edge_11.png)

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Ruffles use a lot of yarn
> A 3-dc-per-stitch ruffle triples the stitch count of the edge. Weigh your yarn before starting a wide ruffle.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Start an edging away from a corner
> Join the yarn partway along a side, not at a corner. The join hides better, and you can adjust the last repeat before you reach it.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> A plain blanket with a beautiful border looks like it was planned that way all along.
