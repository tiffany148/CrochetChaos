Start here when something looks wrong. Find the symptom, read the most likely causes in order, and follow the fix or the chapter reference. Most problems come from one of three sources: the count, the tension, or where the hook went in.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Don't fix anything until you know what happened. Look, count, then decide.

## Edges and width

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Piece gets wider row by row | Extra stitch at the start (working into the base of the turning chain when it counts) or at the end | Count each row; mark the first and last stitch |
| Piece gets narrower | Last stitch missed, often the top of the turning chain | Mark the turning chain top; work into it if it counts |
| One edge wavy, other straight | Turning chain too long or too short on one side | Try a different turning chain height on that edge |
| Gaps at the ends of dc rows | Ch-3 turning chain looser than a dc | Use a stacked sc or chainless starting dc (Chapter 18) |
| Foundation edge tighter than the rest | Foundation chain worked too tightly | Larger hook for the chain, or foundation stitches |
| Top edge flares | Last rows worked looser, or extra stitches | Recount; work last rows with a smaller hook |

## Circles, hats and rounds

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Circle ruffles | Too many increases for the stitch and tension | Work an even round, or skip increases (Chapter 25) |
| Circle cups | Too few increases | Add increases |
| Circle becomes a hexagon | Increases stacked in the same place | Stagger the increases |
| Visible seam line drifting | Joined rounds always started at the same point | Turn each round or use a standing start |
| Spiral stair-step at the end | Normal spiral jog | Taper with sl st before fastening off |
| Hat too big | Crown too wide, or loose gauge | Compare crown diameter with the table (Chapter 44) |
| Hat too short or too long | Height measured from the wrong place | Measure from the crown center, flat |
| Magic ring opens up | Tail not secured, slippery yarn | Tighten again, weave in two directions (Chapter 51) |

## Stitches and texture

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ridges on one row | That row worked in front or back loop only by accident | Frog back to the row and rework |
| Twisted foundation | Chain twisted before the first row or join | Lay the chain flat before joining; work into the back bumps |
| Bobbles on the wrong side | Worked on the wrong row | Bobbles pop to the side facing away; work them on WS rows |
| Puffs lopsided | Loops drawn up to different heights | Pull all loops to the same height before closing |
| Post-stitch ribbing too tight | Post stitches pull in | More stitches or a larger hook |
| Holes in amigurumi | Hook too large, loose tension | Go down a hook size; use the invisible decrease |
| Fabric too stiff | Hook too small for the yarn | Go up a hook size and swatch |
| Fabric too floppy | Hook too large | Go down a hook size and swatch |

## Color

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Color blips at changes | New color brought in on the wrong yarn-over | Change on the final yarn-over of the stitch before (Chapter 11) |
| Carried yarn shows through | Light color over dark carry, or open stitches | Tighter stitches; carry only where needed; center sc |
| Tapestry motif leans | Normal slant of sc in rounds | Center sc, or work in rows (Chapter 58) |
| Stripes jog in rounds | Normal jog in rounds | Use a jogless change or start each round with a standing stitch |
| Yarns tangle in colorwork | Turning the same way every row | Alternate turning direction |
| Dye runs in wash | Saturated colors releasing dye | Wash a test swatch with a white scrap first |

## Size and fit

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Finished piece smaller than planned | Gauge tighter than the swatch, or swatch not washed | Re-measure gauge in the project (Chapter 16) |
| Garment grew after washing | Fiber relaxed or heavy fabric stretched | Dry flat; choose lighter stitches; swatch-wash next time |
| Sleeves unequal | Different rows or tension | Work sleeves at the same time; count rows |
| Neckline won't pass over head | Opening too small; band too tight | Rework the band looser; check before finishing (Chapter 41) |
| Pet garment rubs | Openings in the wrong place or too small | Remeasure on the standing pet (Chapter 48) |

## Yarn and materials

| Symptom | Most likely causes | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Yarn splitting | Pointed hook head, loosely plied yarn | Rounder hook; slow down; insert carefully |
| Yarn kinking or over-twisting | Crochet adds or removes twist | Let the work dangle to untwist |
| Running out of yarn | Underestimated amount | Weigh the project as you go (Chapter 68) |
| New ball looks different | Different dye lot | Alternate two balls for a few rows to blend |
| Fuzzy yarn won't frog | Mohair or brushed fibers locking | Tease out slowly; swatch first next time |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Most "mystery" problems are counts
> Before changing hooks, yarn or patterns, count the last three rows. A count error explains far more problems than bad luck.
