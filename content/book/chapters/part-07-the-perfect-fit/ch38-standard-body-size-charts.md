These charts reproduce the Craft Yarn Council's standard body measurements (craftyarncouncil.com, checked November 9, 2026). **They are actual body measurements, not finished garment sizes**: add ease from the fit chart below to get a finished measurement. All figures are in inches; multiply by 2.54 for centimetres. Two published rows (back waist length and cross back) are left out here; see the CYC site for them. Head circumferences are in Chapter 43.

> [!HINT] How to use a standard chart
> Measure the actual person first (Chapter 37). Use the chart to fill gaps, to grade sizes for a pattern, or when making a gift for someone you cannot measure. Real bodies differ from averages.

## Choosing ease (CYC fit guidelines)

| Fit | Ease relative to actual bust/chest |
|---|---|
| Very close fitting (negative ease) | About 2–4 in (5–10 cm) less than the actual bust/chest |
| Close fitting (zero ease) | The actual bust/chest measurement |
| Classic fit (some positive ease) | About 2–4 in (5–10 cm) more |
| Loose fit | About 4–6 in (10–15 cm) more |
| Oversized | About 6 in (15 cm) or more |

## Length guidelines (CYC)

| Wearer | Hip length | Tunic length |
|---|---|---|
| Child | About 2 in (5 cm) below the waist | About 6 in (15 cm) below the waist |
| Woman | About 6 in (15 cm) below the waist | About 11 in (28 cm) below the waist |
| Man | Usually within 1–2 in (2.5–5 cm) of the actual back hip length | — |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Size by chest, then check the arms
> Choose a size by the chest or bust measurement plus your ease, then check upper arm and length for the same size. Adjust those separately if they don't match.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Babies grow faster than you crochet
> For baby gifts, choose the next size up. A 6-month sweater given to a newborn will fit in time; a newborn sweater may never be worn.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> A chart tells you about most people. A tape measure tells you about your person.


> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Remove items when unattended
> Take crochet garments off pets that are left alone, so nothing can catch or be chewed.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Your pet will love the sweater most if it smells like home. Wash it with your usual detergent before the first wear, if the pet tolerates it.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Some pets hate clothes
> Not every pet tolerates a garment. If the pet freezes, hides or scratches at it, take it off. Their comfort comes first.

> [!HINT] Measure pets twice
> Pets move. Take each measurement twice, on different days if possible, and use the larger chest measurement.
