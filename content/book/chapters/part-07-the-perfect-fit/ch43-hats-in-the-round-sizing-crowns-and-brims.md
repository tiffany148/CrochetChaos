A hat is the perfect project for learning to work in the round. It is small enough to finish in an evening or two, it teaches the geometry of circles and tubes, and it can be tried on as you go. This chapter turns hat making into a method rather than a recipe, so you can make a hat for any head in any yarn, top down or bottom up, and know why it fits.

![hat_schematic (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/hat_schematic.png)

## The three measurements that matter

**Head circumference.** Measure around the fullest part of the head: across the forehead, just above the ears, and around the back of the skull at its widest. Keep the tape snug but not tight. Measure twice.

**Hat height.** Measure from the top center of the crown, over the head, to where the brim should sit. For a fitted beanie that is often around the middle of the earlobe; for a hat with a folded brim, add the fold depth; for a slouchy hat, add the slouch you want. Measure the wearer rather than relying on an age chart.

**Finished circumference.** Most crochet hats are made smaller than the head so the fabric stretches gently and stays on. That difference is called negative ease. Designers commonly plan about 1 to 2 inches (2.5 to 5 cm) of negative ease for an adult hat: more for stretchy fabric such as many acrylics or post-stitch ribbing, less for firmer fabric such as cotton or dense single crochet. Treat that as a starting point and confirm it with your swatch: stretch a 4-inch section of the fabric and see how far it goes and how well it recovers.

## The crown: a flat circle with a stopping rule

A top-down hat starts as a flat circle. You increase evenly each round until the circle is wide enough, then stop increasing and work even rounds to form the tube of the hat body.

**The stopping rule.** Stop increasing when the diameter of the flat circle is about the finished circumference divided by π (3.14). A 20-inch finished hat needs a crown about 20 ÷ 3.14 = 6.37 inches across. Measure the crown flat, without stretching, straight across through the center.

| 14 in | 4.46 in | 35.6 cm | 11.3 cm |

These values are exact calculations (circumference ÷ π), rounded to two decimals in inches and one in centimeters.

**The stitch-count rule.** The other way to know when to stop is to count. Multiply the finished circumference by your stitch gauge in stitches per inch. At 3 dc per inch (12 dc in 4 inches), a 20-inch hat needs 20 × 3 = 60 stitches around. Round to a number that suits your increase pattern, then check the diameter as well; the two methods should agree within a round.

### Increase schedules by stitch height

Taller stitches need more increases per round to stay flat, because each round adds more to the radius. These common starting schedules are widely used; your tension may need a small adjustment.

| Stitch | Round 1 (in ring) | Increase each round | Round *n* stitch count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single crochet | 6 sc | +6 | 6*n* |
| Half double crochet | 8 hdc | +8 | 8*n* |
| Double crochet | 12 dc (or ch 3 + 11 dc) | +12 | 12*n* |

![loop_dc (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/loop_dc.png)

**How the increases are placed (double crochet).** Round 2: 2 dc in each st (24). Round 3: *2 dc in next st, dc in next st; rep from * around (36). Round 4: *2 dc in next st, dc in next 2 sts; rep (48). Round 5: *2 dc in next st, dc in next 3 sts; rep (60). In each round, the number of plain stitches between increases goes up by one. The same logic applies to single crochet with 6 increases and to half double crochet with 8.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I’d Known That!
> If the crown ruffles like a pie crust, you have too many increases for your stitch height and tension; work an even round, or skip an increase round. If it cups into a bowl too early, you need more increases; add one.
>
> Stagger the increases. Working the increase in the same position every round makes a hexagon or a pinwheel. On alternate rounds, start with half of the plain stitches, then the increase.
>
> Measure the crown before you start the body. It takes thirty seconds and saves you from a hat that fits a grapefruit or a pumpkin.

## The body: work even

Once the crown has the right diameter or count, stop increasing. Work one stitch in each stitch around. The hat now grows straight down as a tube. Measure height from the center of the crown down the side, with the hat lying flat, and stop when you reach the planned height minus the brim depth.

**Joined rounds or spiral?** Joined rounds with a turning chain make a visible seam line that is easy to place at the back. Spirals hide the seam but require a moving marker. In double crochet, many crocheters prefer joined rounds because tall stitches in a spiral leave a noticeable step.

**Turning or not.** Working joined rounds without turning tends to make the seam line slant, because the stitches lean slightly. Turning at the end of each joined round alternates the right and wrong side and keeps the seam vertical. Both are fine; choose one deliberately.

## A worked example (calculated, not test-crocheted)

> [!PATTERN] Everyday Double Crochet Beanie (example calculation)
> **Target:** head 22 in; negative ease 2 in; finished circumference 20 in; height 8 in including a 1.5-in brim. These are illustrative choices.
>
> **Example gauge:** 12 dc = 4 in (3 sts per in); 1 dc round ≈ 0.5 in tall in this example. Use your own swatch numbers.
>
> **Crown:** Rnds 1–5 as above: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 dc. Check: 60 ÷ 3 = 20 in circumference; crown should measure about 6.37 in across.
>
> **Body:** work even (60 dc) until the hat measures 6.5 in from the crown center.
>
> **Brim:** sl st to join; ch 2, *FPdc around next st, BPdc around next st; rep from * around; join. Repeat for about 1.5 in. (60 sts; an even number keeps the rib pattern continuous.)
>
> **Status:** stitch counts and dimensions are arithmetic from the stated example gauge. This hat has not been physically crocheted or fit-tested for this edition.

## Brims and bands

| Brim | How it is made | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Post-stitch rib | Alternating FPdc and BPdc around | Stretchy, ribbed look; pulls in |
| BLO single crochet band | Short rows of sc BLO worked sideways, then joined around the hat edge | Very stretchy, horizontal ribs |
| Folded brim | Work the brim twice the depth and fold it up | Warm double layer over the ears |
| Plain sc or crab stitch edge | One or two rounds of sc, or reverse sc | Neat finish for a fitted hat |

## Bottom-up hats

You can also start at the brim and finish at the crown. Make a band or foundation round to the finished circumference, join, and work even to the start of the crown. Then reverse the crown schedule: for a 60-stitch double crochet hat, decrease 12 stitches each round (*dc in next 3 sts, dc2tog; rep* gives 48; then *dc in next 2, dc2tog* gives 36; and so on). When about 12 stitches remain, cut a long tail, weave it through the remaining stitches, pull it closed and secure.

Bottom-up hats are easy to size because the band can be wrapped around the head before you commit. Top-down hats are easy to adjust in height and use every last bit of yarn.

## Variations that grow from the same method

- **Slouchy hat:** continue increasing a round or two beyond the stopping rule, work even for the extra length, then decrease back to the finished circumference at the brim.
- **Beret:** increase to a disk noticeably wider than the head-size crown (choose the width from a sketch or a beret you like), work a few even rounds, then decrease sharply into a snug band.
- **Earflaps:** work two flaps in rows from the lower edge, centered on each side, with the back gap wider than the front gap. Position them by trying the hat on.
- **Pompom:** sew it to the crown with a strong double strand, through the center ring; a heavy pompom on a loose crown will pull the top into a point.
- **Two-color:** change color at the join of a round on the last yarn over (Chapter 11); carry the unused color inside the hat for narrow stripes.

> [!GRANNY] Granny’s Wisdom
> Make the first hat for yourself. You can try it on every round, you will learn what the measurements mean on a real head, and you will forgive its little mistakes. The second hat, made for someone you love, will be the one that fits.

> [!SAFETY]
> Hats for newborns and premature infants are often requested by hospitals and charities with specific rules about fiber, size and decorations. Avoid loose embellishments, long ties and small parts on hats for babies and young children.
