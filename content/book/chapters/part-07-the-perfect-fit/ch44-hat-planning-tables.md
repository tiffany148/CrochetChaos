This chapter turns the method of Chapter 43 into look-up tables. Pick a finished circumference, find your gauge, and read off the stitch count, the number of increase rounds and the crown size. All values are arithmetic only, produced by computer.

> [!STATUS]
> Calculated tables, not tested hats. Finished circumference is your choice: head circumference (CYC chart in Chapter 43) minus the negative ease you choose after stretching your swatch. Always make a gauge swatch in the stitch and yarn of the hat.

## Head sizes and example finished circumferences

CYC head-circumference ranges, with examples of 1 in and 2 in negative ease subtracted from each end of the range. Chapter 43 describes 1–2 in as a common starting point for adult hats; for babies and children, choose ease from your swatch's stretch and try the hat on as you go.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Rounding to the schedule changes the size a little
> If your gauge gives 63 dc and the schedule rounds to 60, the hat comes out about 1 in smaller (3 sts ÷ 3 per in). Check what the rounded count gives before you start: count ÷ gauge = finished circumference.

## Round-by-round crown schedules

The standard increase sequences up to each stopping count. In round *n* of a dc crown there are *n* − 2 plain stitches between increases (*2 dc in next st, dc in next n − 2 sts*); for hdc and sc the same rule applies, with 8 or 6 repeats.

### DC crown

| Round | Instruction | Stitches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 dc in ring (or ch 3 + 11 dc) | 12 |
| 2 | 2 dc in each st around | 24 |

| 4 | *2 dc in next st, dc in next 2 sts; rep from * 12 times | 48 |
| 5 | *2 dc in next st, dc in next 3 sts; rep from * 12 times | 60 |
| 6 | *2 dc in next st, dc in next 4 sts; rep from * 12 times | 72 |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — The plain-stitch number is the round number minus two
> Round 5 of a dc crown: 2 dc in next st, dc in next 3 sts. Round 8: dc in next 6 sts. Lose your place? Count the stitches between two increases on the last round and add one.

## Bottom-up crown decreases

Reverse of the increase schedule: each round removes one repeat's worth of stitches until about 12 (dc), 8 (hdc) or 6 (sc) remain. Then cut a long tail and gather the last stitches closed.

### DC hat body

| Body stitches | Stitch count after each decrease round | Decrease rounds |
|---|---|---|
| 48 | 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 | 3 |
| 60 | 60 → 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 | 4 |
| 72 | 72 → 60 → 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 | 5 |
| 84 | 84 → 72 → 60 → 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 | 6 |
| 96 | 96 → 84 → 72 → 60 → 48 → 36 → 24 → 12 | 7 |

### HDC hat body

| Body stitches | Stitch count after each decrease round | Decrease rounds |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | 32 → 24 → 16 → 8 | 3 |
| 40 | 40 → 32 → 24 → 16 → 8 | 4 |
| 48 | 48 → 40 → 32 → 24 → 16 → 8 | 5 |
| 56 | 56 → 48 → 40 → 32 → 24 → 16 → 8 | 6 |
| 64 | 64 → 56 → 48 → 40 → 32 → 24 → 16 → 8 | 7 |

### SC hat body

| Body stitches | Stitch count after each decrease round | Decrease rounds |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | 24 → 18 → 12 → 6 | 3 |
| 30 | 30 → 24 → 18 → 12 → 6 | 4 |
| 36 | 36 → 30 → 24 → 18 → 12 → 6 | 5 |
| 42 | 42 → 36 → 30 → 24 → 18 → 12 → 6 | 6 |
| 48 | 48 → 42 → 36 → 30 → 24 → 18 → 12 → 6 | 7 |

For a body of 60 dc, the first decrease round is *dc in next 3 sts, dc2tog; rep from * 12 times (48), then *dc in next 2 sts, dc2tog* (36), *dc in next st, dc2tog* (24), and dc2tog around (12).

> [!HINT] Hat height
> Measure height from the crown to the bottom edge with the hat lying flat, and compare it with the hat height you measured from the top center of the head to where the brim should sit (Chapter 43). Add any brim or fold-up band to that length.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Make the crown, then try it on like a little cap. If it sits flat on top of the head, you have stopped increasing at the right time.
