The finishing details are where a crocheted garment or bag starts to look professional: buttons that stay done up, buttonholes that line up, zippers that lie flat, linings that stop a bag from stretching, and straps that carry weight. This chapter covers the crochet methods for each and the sewing you will need alongside them.

> [!STATUS]
> Standard methods, described for this edition. Hardware sizes depend on your yarn and fabric; always test a closure on your swatch before committing a garment to it.

## Buttonbands

A buttonband is a strip of firm fabric along an opening that holds the buttons on one side and the buttonholes on the other. In crochet it is usually worked directly onto the edge of the garment in rows of sc, hdc or sc rib.

1. Join the yarn at the bottom of the edge (or the top, as your pattern says).
2. Work one row of sc evenly along the edge. A common starting ratio along row ends is about 1 sc per sc row and 2 sc per dc row; adjust so the band lies flat without rippling or pulling in.
3. Work further rows of sc (or sc BLO for a ribbed look) until the band is as wide as you want.
4. Work the buttonholes in the matching row on the other band (see below).

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Make the button band first
> Work the button band first, sew on the buttons, and then work the buttonhole band, placing each hole opposite a button. It is far easier than calculating hole spacing in the air.

## Buttonholes

| Method | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Chain-space hole | In a band row, ch 2 (or the number needed), skip the same number of stitches, and continue. On the next row, work the same number of sc into the chain space | Most buttons; size by the number of chains |
| Natural gap | Push the button between two tall stitches (dc or tr) | Open or lacy fabric; small buttons |
| Loop | Ch a loop at the edge large enough for the button, sl st back into the edge | Single buttons, toggles, edges without a band |
| Eyelet | Work (ch 1, sk 1) in a single row | Very small buttons and ties |

> [!HINT] Sizing a chain-space hole
> Make the hole on your swatch first. It should be a snug fit for the button, because crochet stretches; a hole that fits loosely when new will gape in wear.

### Spacing buttonholes

Mark the top and bottom button positions first, then divide the distance between them evenly. If there are 5 buttons, there are 4 equal gaps between the outer two. Count the stitches between the markers on the button band and divide by the number of gaps; if it does not divide evenly, spread the extra stitches across the gaps.

> [!HINT] Worked example
> 61 stitches between the centers of the top and bottom buttons, 5 buttons, so 4 gaps: 61 ÷ 4 = 15.25. Make three gaps of 15 stitches and one of 16 (15 + 15 + 15 + 16 = 61).

## Sewing on buttons

- Use a strong thread or a split strand of the project yarn, if it is smooth and strong.
- Sew through the band, not just the surface, so the button is anchored.
- For thick fabric, make a thread shank: hold a matchstick or toothpick between the button and the fabric while sewing, then remove it, wrap the thread around the stitches under the button several times and tie off. The shank leaves room for the buttonhole band.
- A small, flat button on the inside, sewn through with the outer button, spreads the load on soft fabric.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Take your finished button band to the shop and try buttons on it there. A button that looked perfect in the tray can be too heavy once it sits on wool.

## Zippers in crochet

Zippers work well in firm crochet such as sc fabric. The edge must be straight and the same length as the zipper's teeth area, or the fabric will ripple.

1. Work a firm sc edge along both sides of the opening and block the piece.
2. Close the zipper and pin it behind the opening so the crochet edges just meet over the teeth. Pin from the center outward.
3. Hand-sew with a backstitch through the crochet, close to the edge but clear of the teeth, using sewing thread.
4. Whipstitch the zipper tape edges to the wrong side of the fabric.
5. Open the zipper and check that the fabric does not catch in the teeth.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Never stretch the crochet to fit the zipper
> If the edge is longer than the zipper, ease it evenly with many pins; if it is shorter, add a row to the edge or buy a longer zipper. A stretched edge waves for the life of the garment.

## Linings for bags and baskets

A fabric lining stops crochet from stretching, keeps small items from slipping through stitches, and hides the wrong side.

1. Measure the finished crochet bag inside: width, depth, and base.
2. Cut lining fabric to those sizes plus a seam allowance on each edge (commonly about ½ in).
3. Sew the lining into a bag shape, with any pockets added first.
4. Fold the top edge of the lining under and slip it inside the crochet bag, wrong sides together.
5. Hand-sew the folded edge just below the crochet top edge with small stitches.

> [!HINT] Line the handles too
> For heavy use, sew a strip of cotton webbing or ribbon along the inside of each handle. It carries the weight so the crochet does not stretch.

## Straps and handles

| Strap type | Method | Stretch |
|---|---|---|
| Sc strip | Rows of sc, 4–8 sts wide | Some; firmer in cotton |
| Folded strip | Make a wide strip and seam it lengthwise into a tube | Less stretch, more body |
| Slip-stitch cord | Rows of sl st in the back loop | Low stretch, dense |
| Crochet over cord | Encase rope or cord in sc | Very little stretch |
| Webbing or leather | Sewn or riveted to a firm edge | None |

> [!SAFETY] Load and wear
> A strap's strength depends on the fiber, stitch, joins and hardware. Test any bag strap gradually with weight before relying on it, and check the joins for wear.

## Drawstrings, ties and toggles

- **Eyelet rounds** (dc, ch 1, sk 1) make an easy channel for a drawstring at the top of a bag.
- **Chain cords** stretch; for a drawstring, a slip-stitch cord or a crocheted sc strip holds its length better.
- **Toggles** need a loop sized like a buttonhole.

> [!SAFETY] Cords on children's and baby items
> Do not add drawstrings or long ties at the neck or hood of children's garments; many countries restrict them because they can catch. For baby items, avoid long cords and loose loops.

## Snaps, hooks and magnetic clasps

Sew-on snaps and hook-and-eye fasteners work on firm crochet; sew them through a strip of grosgrain ribbon or fabric sewn to the inside if the crochet is loose. Magnetic bag clasps usually need a backing washer; fit them through a fabric lining or a small piece of interfacing rather than directly through crochet, so the prongs do not pull out.

## Labels

Sewing a care label inside a gift or a sold item helps its owner. A label might give fiber content and how to wash it. Rules for labels on items for sale vary by country; check the rules that apply where you sell.
