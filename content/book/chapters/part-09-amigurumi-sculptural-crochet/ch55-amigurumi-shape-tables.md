Amigurumi shapes follow simple stitch-count rules: a flat disk grows 6 stitches per round, a tube keeps its count, and a dome or ball closes by removing 6 per round. This chapter sets those rules out as round-by-round tables for spheres, ovals, cylinders and cones. All tables were generated by computer from the rules shown.

> [!STATUS]
> Calculated schedules, not tested toys. The number of even rounds in a sphere varies between designers and with tension; the tables use one common choice (even rounds = increase rounds − 1). Stuff firmly and compare shape as you go.

## Ball (sphere) schedules

A ball needs about as many rounds to go up as to come down, with a short straight band in the middle. Increase rounds use (sc in next k sts, inc) 6 times; decrease rounds mirror them with invisible decreases (Chapter 22). Stagger the increases on alternate rounds to keep the ball round.

## Ovals and capsules

For an egg or capsule, add more even rounds to the middle of any ball schedule. Each extra even round adds roughly one row height to the length. For a capsule worked from a chain, see the method in Chapter 54.

## Cylinders (tubes with a flat base)

Work a flat disk to the base size, then one round in the back loop only to turn the edge, then even rounds.

| Base stitches | Disk (increase rounds) | Turn the edge | Sides: sc in each st |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | Rounds 1–3 | Round 4 (BLO) | Rounds 5 on |
| 24 | Rounds 1–4 | Round 5 (BLO) | Rounds 6 on |
| 30 | Rounds 1–5 | Round 6 (BLO) | Rounds 7 on |
| 36 | Rounds 1–6 | Round 7 (BLO) | Rounds 8 on |
| 48 | Rounds 1–8 | Round 9 (BLO) | Rounds 10 on |
| 60 | Rounds 1–10 | Round 11 (BLO) | Rounds 12 on |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — One BLO round makes a flat bottom
> That single back-loop round is what makes a pot, basket or foot stand up. Without it, the base curves gently into the sides.

## Cones

A cone rises from a point because it grows more slowly than a flat disk. Three common rates, all starting with 6 sc in a ring:

| Round | +6 every other round | +3 every round | Start 4, +2 every round |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 6 | 4 |
| 2 | 6 | 9 | 6 |
| 3 | 12 | 12 | 8 |
| 4 | 12 | 15 | 10 |
| 5 | 18 | 18 | 12 |
| 6 | 18 | 21 | 14 |
| 7 | 24 | 24 | 16 |
| 8 | 24 | 27 | 18 |
| 9 | 30 | 30 | 20 |
| 10 | 30 | 33 | 22 |
| 11 | 36 | 36 | 24 |
| 12 | 36 | 39 | 26 |

The slower the growth, the steeper and narrower the cone. For an ear or horn, use +2 or +3 per round; for a gentle dome, increase every other round. Spread the increases evenly around each round.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Write the round number on a sticky note and move your marker every round. Amigurumi forgives many things, but not a lost count.


> [!HINT] Check the chart orientation
> Before starting a chart, check whether it is shown from the right side, and whether row 1 is at the bottom. Most are, but not all.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Colorwork is slow on the first row and quick on the tenth. Give it ten rows.
