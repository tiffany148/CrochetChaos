Every chart in this library works for three methods: **tapestry crochet** (one square = one sc), **corner-to-corner** (one square = one tile) and **filet** (filled square = motif, open square = background). The square counts beside each chart were computed from the chart itself, so you can estimate the share of each color.

> [!STATUS]
> Charts are original designs drawn for this edition (simple geometric and block-letter motifs). They were checked by computer for consistent row lengths and color counts; none has been stitched for this edition. Remember that crochet stitches are not perfectly square, so finished motifs may look slightly taller or wider than the chart (Chapter 56).

## How to use a chart in each method

| Method | One square equals | Reading direction | Tips |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tapestry (rows) | 1 sc | RS rows right to left, WS rows left to right | Change color on the last yarn-over of the stitch before |
| Tapestry (rounds) | 1 sc | Every round right to left | Expect a slight lean; center sc reduces it |
| C2C | 1 tile | Diagonally, corner to corner | Use bobbins for each color block |
| Filet | 1 mesh square | As tapestry rows | Filled = 3 dc; open = dc, ch 2 |

> [!HINT] Spacing words
> Allow one background column between letters and three between words. A five-letter word at 5 squares per letter needs 5 × 5 + 4 = 29 squares across, plus a border on each side.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Chart words on graph paper first
> Before stitching a name, chart it in full on graph paper or a spreadsheet. Letters that look fine alone can crowd each other or leave a lopsided gap together.
