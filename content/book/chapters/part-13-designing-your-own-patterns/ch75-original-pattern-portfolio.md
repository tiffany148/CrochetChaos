These original patterns put the techniques of this book to work. They are small on purpose, so you can finish one in an evening and see the method in your own hands.

> [!STATUS] Testing status of every pattern in this chapter
> These patterns were written and calculated for this edition. Stitch counts were checked by arithmetic and, where noted, by computer. **None of them has been test-crocheted or tech-edited by an independent tester yet.** Finished sizes are estimates from the stated gauge. Make a swatch, and treat the first make as a test.

## Pattern 1: Everyday Half Double Washcloth

A first project that teaches turning chains, counting and straight edges.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted-weight (CYC 4) cotton, about 50–60 yd (estimate; not weighed from a sample) |
| Hook | 5 mm (H-8), or the size that gives gauge |
| Gauge (target) | 14 hdc and about 10 rows = 4 in (10 cm) |
| Estimated size | About 8.3 in wide (29 sts ÷ 3.5 sts per in); work until square |
| Notions | Tapestry needle |

> [!PATTERN] Everyday Half Double Washcloth
> Ch 31.
> Row 1: Hdc in 3rd ch from hook (skipped ch do not count as a st) and in each ch across. (29 hdc)
> Row 2: Ch 2 (does not count as a st), turn. Hdc in each st across. (29 hdc)
> Rep Row 2 until the piece measures the same as its width.
> Edging: Ch 1, work sc evenly around all four sides, with 3 sc in each corner. Join with sl st to first sc. Fasten off and weave in ends.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Count only the first and last stitch
> If your edges wander, check that you worked into the very first stitch (right next to the turning chain) and the very last. Those two stitches are where most counts go wrong.

## Pattern 2: Ribbed Ear Warmer

A sideways-worked band of back-loop single crochet, which makes a stretchy rib.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted-weight (CYC 4) wool or wool blend; amount depends on size (not weighed from a sample) |
| Hook | 5.5 mm (I-9) |
| Gauge (target) | 14 sc = 4 in across the stitches |
| Estimated width | About 3.4 in (12 sts ÷ 3.5 sts per in) |
| Sizes | Fit by trying on, using the head charts in Chapter {ch:hats-in-the-round} |

> [!PATTERN] Ribbed Ear Warmer
> Ch 13.
> Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (12 sc)
> Row 2: Ch 1 (does not count as a st), turn. Sc in the back loop only of each st across. (12 sc)
> Rep Row 2 until the band, slightly stretched, fits snugly around the head over the ears. End after a WS row.
> Joining: Hold the first and last rows together and sl st through both layers across. Fasten off, leaving a long tail.
> Optional cinch: Wrap the tail several times around the seam to gather it, and secure on the inside.

> [!HINT] How snug is snug?
> The band should stay put without squeezing. Try it on before joining. If it slides off, remove a few rows; if it leaves a mark, add a few.

## Pattern 3: Tapestry Heart Coaster

A small chart for practicing tapestry single crochet in rows. It uses the 12 × 12 heart chart from Chapter {ch:tapestry-masterclass}: 78 squares in color A and 66 in color B (counted by computer from the chart).

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Advanced beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted-weight (CYC 4) cotton: color A (heart) and color B (background) |
| Hook | 4.5 mm (7) |
| Gauge (target) | 14 sc = 4 in |
| Estimated size | About 3.4 in square before the border; about 4 in with the border |

**Reading this pattern:** one square of the chart = one sc. Row 1 is the bottom row of the chart. Right-side rows read right to left; wrong-side rows read left to right. Carry the unused color inside the stitches throughout (Chapter {ch:tapestry-masterclass}). Change color on the last yarn-over of the stitch before the new color.

> [!PATTERN] Tapestry Heart Coaster
> With B, ch 13.
> Row 1 is worked into the chain: sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across, carrying A from the first stitch. (12 sc)
> Row 1 is the first line of the sequence below (all B). For Rows 2–12: ch 1 (does not count), turn, and work each row's color sequence.

1. Row 1 (RS): 12 B. (12 sc)
2. Row 2 (WS): 5 B, 2 A, 5 B. (12 sc)
3. Row 3 (RS): 4 B, 4 A, 4 B. (12 sc)
4. Row 4 (WS): 3 B, 6 A, 3 B. (12 sc)
5. Row 5 (RS): 2 B, 8 A, 2 B. (12 sc)
6. Row 6 (WS): 1 B, 10 A, 1 B. (12 sc)
7. Row 7 (RS): 12 A. (12 sc)
8. Row 8 (WS): 12 A. (12 sc)
9. Row 9 (RS): 12 A. (12 sc)
10. Row 10 (WS): 1 B, 4 A, 2 B, 4 A, 1 B. (12 sc)
11. Row 11 (RS): 2 B, 2 A, 4 B, 2 A, 2 B. (12 sc)
12. Row 12 (WS): 12 B. (12 sc)

> [!PATTERN] Border
> With B, ch 1, do not turn. Work sc around the edge: 3 sc in each corner stitch, 1 sc in each stitch along the top and bottom, and 1 sc in each row end along the sides, skipping a row end if needed to keep the edge flat. Join with sl st to the first sc. (About 52–56 sc; the exact count is not critical.) Fasten off. Weave in ends inside same-color stitches.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — The heart may lean
> Tapestry single crochet slants slightly because each stitch sits a little to one side of the one below. Working the rows back and forth, as here, reduces the lean compared with rounds.

## Pattern 4: C2C Heart Panel

The same 12 × 12 heart chart worked corner to corner. One chart square = one tile.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Intermediate |
| Yarn | Worsted-weight (CYC 4): color A and color B; amounts depend on tile size |
| Hook | 5 mm (H-8) |
| Tile count | 144 tiles: 78 A, 66 B |
| Diagonal rows | 23 (12 increasing, 11 decreasing) |
| Use | A pillow front, a bag panel, or a square for a sampler blanket |

> [!PATTERN] C2C Heart Panel
> Begin at the bottom right corner of the chart with B.
> Rows 1–12: increase one tile on each edge every row, following the chart along each diagonal.
> Rows 13–23: decrease one tile on each edge every row, following the chart.
> Change color within a row on the last yarn-over before the new tile. Use separate small bobbins for each area of A on the wide middle diagonals.
> Finish with a round of sc in B, 3 sc in each corner, and block flat.

> [!HINT] Mark the chart as you go
> Highlight each diagonal on a copy of the chart when it is finished. With C2C, the next diagonal starts on the opposite edge, so it is easy to lose your place.

## Pattern 5: Practice Amigurumi Ball

The basic sphere behind most amigurumi heads and bodies. Stitch counts were checked by computer: each increase round adds 6 stitches, and each decrease round removes 6.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner amigurumi |
| Yarn | Worsted-weight (CYC 4) cotton or acrylic |
| Hook | 3.5–4 mm (E-4 to G-6): a hook smaller than the label suggests, for a fabric with no gaps |
| Notions | Stitch marker, fiberfill, tapestry needle |
| Estimated size | About 2 in across (30 sts at an assumed 5 sts per in gives about 6 in around; 6 ÷ 3.14 ≈ 1.9 in). Your size depends on your gauge. |

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p13-market-tote.jpg)

> [!PATTERN] Practice Amigurumi Ball (worked in a spiral; do not join; move the marker up each round)
> Rnd 1: 6 sc in a magic ring. (6)
> Rnd 2: Inc in each st around. (12)
> Rnd 3: (Sc in next st, inc) 6 times. (18)
> Rnd 4: (Sc in next 2 sts, inc) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 5: (Sc in next 3 sts, inc) 6 times. (30)
> Rnds 6–10: Sc in each st around. (30)
> Rnd 11: (Sc in next 3 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 12: (Sc in next 2 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (18)
> Begin stuffing firmly.
> Rnd 13: (Sc in next st, inv dec) 6 times. (12)
> Finish stuffing.
> Rnd 14: Inv dec 6 times. (6)
> Fasten off, leaving a tail. Thread the tail through the front loops of the last 6 stitches, pull to close, and hide the tail inside the ball.

> [!SAFETY] For toys meant for babies and young children
> Do not use buttons, beads or safety eyes on toys for children under three; embroider features instead. Secure every tail, and check the toy regularly for loose stitches. This book does not certify any toy as safe; follow the toy-safety rules that apply where you live if you plan to sell toys.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Stuff a little more than you think you need. Fiberfill settles, and a firm ball keeps its shape.

## Pattern 6: Everyday Ribbed Beanie

A top-down beanie in half double crochet with a post-stitch ribbed brim. It uses the hdc schedule from Chapter 44.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Advanced beginner |
| Yarn | DK to light worsted (CYC 3–4) wool or wool blend; amount not weighed from a sample |
| Hook | 4.5 mm (7), or the size that gives gauge |
| Gauge (target) | 14 hdc = 4 in (3.5 hdc per in) worked in rounds |
| Finished size (calculated) | 72 sts ÷ 3.5 = about 20.6 in around; crown about 6.5 in across (20.6 ÷ π) |
| Fits (planning) | Adult heads of about 21–23 in (CYC "Woman" range), giving about 0.4–2.4 in negative ease |

> [!PATTERN] Everyday Ribbed Beanie (joined rounds; ch 1 at the start of each round does not count as a stitch)
> Rnd 1: Magic ring. Ch 1, 8 hdc in ring. Join with sl st to first hdc. (8)
> Rnd 2: Ch 1, 2 hdc in each st around. Join. (16)
> Rnd 3: Ch 1, *2 hdc in next st, hdc in next st; rep from * 8 times. Join. (24)
> Rnds 4–9: Ch 1, *2 hdc in next st, hdc in next (n − 2) sts; rep from * 8 times, where n is the round number. Join. (Rnd 4: 32 · Rnd 5: 40 · Rnd 6: 48 · Rnd 7: 56 · Rnd 8: 64 · Rnd 9: 72)
> Check: the crown should measure about 6.5 in across. If it is larger or smaller, see the hint below before continuing.
> Rnds 10 on: Ch 1, hdc in each st around. Join. (72) Work even until the hat measures your planned height minus 1.5 in from the center of the crown.
> Brim rounds: Ch 2 (does not count), *FPhdc around next st, BPhdc around next st; rep from * around. Join. (72) Repeat this round until the brim measures 1.5 in. Fasten off and weave in ends.

> [!HINT] If the crown is the wrong size
> A bigger crown means your gauge is looser than 3.5 hdc per in. Either change hook size and start again, or stop at a round whose count gives the circumference you want (count ÷ your gauge). The Hat Planning Tables list both.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Turn the seam into a feature
> Joined hdc rounds make a visible line where each round starts. Start each round one stitch to the left of the last start, or turn the work at the end of every round, to keep the seam straight and tidy.

## Pattern 7: Granny Stripe Throw

The Granny Stripe from the Stitch Pattern Library, sized to any width.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) acrylic or wool blend in several colors; estimate amounts by weighing a swatch (Chapter 68) |
| Hook | 5 mm (H-8) |
| Width | Your choice, in a chain multiple of 3 + 1 |

**Sizing by arithmetic:** make a swatch in the stitch, measure how many groups fit in 4 in, and work out stitches per inch (each group is 3 stitches). **Example (arithmetic only):** if 4 groups measure 4 in, that is 12 stitches over 4 in = 3 stitches per inch, so a 40-in throw needs about 120 stitches. The nearest chain of 3 + 1 is 121.

> [!PATTERN] Granny Stripe Throw
> Ch a multiple of 3 + 1 to the planned width.
> Rows 1–3: Work as Rows 1–3 of the Granny Stripe in the Stitch Pattern Library.
> Repeat Rows 2–3, changing color every two rows (at the end of a row, before turning), until the throw is the planned length.
> Border: Join a border color in any corner. Ch 1, work sc evenly around all edges, with 3 sc in each corner. Join. Work 2 more rounds of sc, with 3 sc in each center corner stitch.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Crochet over your tails as you go
> With a color change every two rows, a granny stripe throw can have hundreds of ends. Lay each new tail along the top of the row and crochet over it for 6–8 stitches.

## Pattern 8: Shell Stitch Baby Blanket

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | DK or worsted (CYC 3–4) machine-washable yarn suitable for baby items |
| Hook | As needed for a soft fabric (often 4.5–5.5 mm with DK–worsted) |
| Width | A chain multiple of 6 + 2 |

**Sizing by arithmetic:** each shell repeat uses 6 chains. Measure one repeat on your swatch (from the center of one sc to the center of the next). **Example (arithmetic only):** if one repeat measures 1.5 in, a 30-in blanket needs 30 ÷ 1.5 = 20 repeats, so chain 20 × 6 + 2 = 122.

> [!PATTERN] Shell Stitch Baby Blanket
> Ch a multiple of 6 + 2.
> Work Rows 1–3 of the Classic Shell Stitch (Stitch Pattern Library), then repeat Rows 2–3 to the planned length, ending after a Row 3.
> Border: Ch 1, sc evenly around, 3 sc in each corner. Join. Optional final round: (sc, ch 3, sl st in the sc just made) every 4th stitch for a picot edge.

> [!SAFETY] Baby items
> Avoid long loose loops, fringe or small parts. Weave all ends in securely. Follow current safe-sleep guidance: loose blankets are generally not recommended in cribs for sleeping infants. Use the blanket for supervised play and tummy time, or in a pram while you are with the baby.

## Pattern 9: Collar-Sleeve Dog Bandana

A triangle that slides onto a flat collar, with no ties around the neck.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) machine-washable cotton or acrylic |
| Hook | 5 mm (H-8) |
| Size | Fit by measurement: the top edge equals the part of the collar you want covered |

> [!PATTERN] Collar-Sleeve Dog Bandana (worked from the point up)
> Row 1: Ch 2, 3 sc in 2nd ch from hook. (3)
> Row 2: Ch 1, turn. 2 sc in first st, sc in each st to last st, 2 sc in last st. (5)
> Rep Row 2 until the top edge measures the width you want. Row n has 2n + 1 stitches.
> Sleeve: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across (no increases). Rep this row until the sleeve section measures twice the collar width plus ½ in.
> Fold the sleeve section to the wrong side and sew its edge to the base of the sleeve, leaving the short ends open. Slide the collar through.

> [!SAFETY] For pets
> A bandana on a collar can still snag. Use it only while you are supervising, choose a breakaway collar where appropriate, and remove it if the dog chews it. Nothing here is a safety device.

## Pattern 10: Solid Granny Cushion Cover

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) cotton or acrylic |
| Hook | 5 mm (H-8) |
| Notions | Square cushion form; tapestry needle |

> [!PATTERN] Solid Granny Cushion Cover
> Make 2 Solid Granny Squares (Motif Library). Work rounds until each square, laid flat and relaxed, is about ½–1 in smaller than the cushion form on each side so the cover fits snugly. Round n has 4n − 1 dc on each side; record your round count so both squares match.
> Hold the squares wrong sides together. Join with sc through both layers along three sides, with 3 sc in each corner. Insert the cushion form. Join the fourth side the same way. Join with sl st and fasten off.

> [!HINT] Removable cover
> For a washable cover, sew a zipper or buttons along the fourth side instead of crocheting it closed (Chapter 26).
