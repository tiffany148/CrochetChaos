## Pattern 11: Little Honey Bear (amigurumi)

All pieces are worked in continuous spiral rounds of sc; move the marker up each round. Counts follow the Amigurumi Shape Tables (Chapter 55).

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Intermediate beginner (amigurumi) |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) cotton or acrylic: main color, small amount of a contrast for the muzzle; black embroidery yarn |
| Hook | 3.5–4 mm, small enough that the fabric shows no gaps |
| Notions | Fiberfill, stitch marker, tapestry needle, long pins |
| Estimated height | About 6–7 in seated (not measured from a sample) |

> [!PATTERN] Head
> Rnd 1: 6 sc in magic ring. (6)
> Rnd 2: Inc in each st around. (12)
> Rnd 3: (Sc in next st, inc) 6 times. (18)
> Rnd 4: (Sc in next 2 sts, inc) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 5: (Sc in next 3 sts, inc) 6 times. (30)
> Rnd 6: (Sc in next 4 sts, inc) 6 times. (36)
> Rnds 7–11: Sc in each st around. (36)
> Rnd 12: (Sc in next 4 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (30)
> Rnd 13: (Sc in next 3 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 14: (Sc in next 2 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (18) Stuff firmly.
> Rnd 15: (Sc in next st, inv dec) 6 times. (12) Finish stuffing.
> Rnd 16: Inv dec 6 times. (6) Fasten off and close the hole.

> [!PATTERN] Body
> Rnds 1–5: As Head Rnds 1–5. (30)
> Rnds 6–12: Sc in each st around. (30)
> Rnd 13: (Sc in next 3 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (24)
> Rnds 14–15: Sc in each st around. (24)
> Rnd 16: (Sc in next 2 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (18)
> Stuff firmly. Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing to the head.

> [!PATTERN] Muzzle (contrast color)
> Rnds 1–3: As Head Rnds 1–3. (18)
> Rnds 4–5: Sc in each st around. (18) Fasten off with a long tail.

> [!PATTERN] Ears (make 2)
> Rnds 1–3: As Head Rnds 1–3. (18)
> Rnds 4–5: Sc in each st around. (18) Fasten off with a long tail. Do not stuff; flatten each ear into a half-circle.

> [!PATTERN] Arms (make 2)
> Rnd 1: 6 sc in magic ring. (6)
> Rnd 2: (Sc in next st, inc) 3 times. (9)
> Rnds 3–10: Sc in each st around. (9)
> Stuff the lower half lightly. Flatten the top and fasten off with a long tail.

> [!PATTERN] Legs (make 2)
> Rnds 1–2: As Head Rnds 1–2. (12)
> Rnds 3–8: Sc in each st around. (12)
> Stuff firmly. Flatten the top and fasten off with a long tail.

> [!PATTERN] Assembly
> Pin every piece in place before sewing (Chapter 53). Sew the head to the top of the body all the way around the body opening. Sew the muzzle low on the face, stuffing it lightly before closing. Sew the ears to the top of the head, the arms to the sides of the body just below the neck, and the legs to the lower front so the bear sits. Embroider the eyes and nose with black yarn.

> [!SAFETY] Toy safety
> Embroider all features; do not use safety eyes, buttons or beads on toys for children under three. Sew every part with many secure stitches, and check the toy often for loose seams. This pattern is not safety-tested and this book does not certify any toy as safe.

## Pattern 12: Round Pet Bed Mat

A flat double-crochet circle that sits in a pet bed or on the floor beside it.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Beginner |
| Yarn | Bulky (CYC 5) machine-washable acrylic or blend |
| Hook | 6.5–8 mm, as needed for a firm flat fabric |
| Size | Fit by diameter: measure the bed floor across |

> [!PATTERN] Round Pet Bed Mat (joined rounds; ch 3 counts as dc)
> Rnd 1: Magic ring. Ch 3, 11 dc in ring. Join. (12)
> Rnd 2: Ch 3, dc in same st, 2 dc in each st around. Join. (24)
> Rnd n (n ≥ 3): Ch 3, work (dc in next n − 2 sts, 2 dc in next st) 12 times in all, counting the ch-3 as the first dc of the first repeat. Join. (12n)
> Continue until the mat measures the bed floor's diameter. Lay it flat every round; if it ruffles, work one round without increases.
> Final round: Ch 1, sc in each st around. Join and fasten off.

> [!SAFETY] Pet items
> Check the mat regularly; remove it if your pet chews or pulls loops out. Do not use it as a heating pad or with a heat source.

## Pattern 13: Tunisian Simple Stitch Scarf

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Advanced beginner (first Tunisian project) |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) wool or blend |
| Hook | Tunisian hook 6–6.5 mm (one or two sizes above the label), long enough to hold 30 loops |
| Width | 30 stitches; width depends on your gauge |

> [!PATTERN] Tunisian Simple Stitch Scarf
> Ch 30.
> Row 1, forward pass: Insert in 2nd ch from hook, yo, draw up a loop and keep it on the hook. Repeat in each ch across. (30 loops on hook)
> Row 1, return pass: Yo, draw through 1 loop; *yo, draw through 2 loops; rep from * until 1 loop remains.
> Row 2 and on, forward pass: Skip the first vertical bar (the loop on the hook is the first stitch). *Insert under the next front vertical bar, yo, draw up a loop; rep from * to the last stitch. Insert under both loops of the last stitch at the edge, draw up a loop. (30 loops)
> Return pass: As Row 1.
> Repeat Row 2 until the scarf is the length you want.
> Bind off: *Insert under the next vertical bar, yo, draw through the bar and the loop on the hook (a sl st); rep from * across. Fasten off.
> Border (optional): One round of sc around all edges, 3 sc in each corner, helps reduce curling.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Tunisian curls; that's normal
> Tunisian simple stitch curls toward the front. A larger hook, a border and a good wet block reduce it.

## Pattern 14: Paw Print Tapestry Block

A square block for a pet-lover's blanket or a pillow front. It uses the paw-print chart from the Chart Library (Chapter 59): 74 squares in A (paw) and 108 in B (background), counted by computer.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Intermediate |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4): A (paw), B (background) |
| Hook | 4.5 mm (7) |
| Gauge (target) | 14 sc = 4 in |
| Estimated size | About 4 × 4 in before border (14 sts ÷ 3.5 per in) |

Work in sc rows, carrying the unused color inside the stitches (Chapter 58). RS rows read right to left, WS rows left to right. Change color on the last yarn-over of the stitch before.

> [!PATTERN] Paw Print Tapestry Block
> With B, ch 15. Row 1 is worked into the chain: sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across (14 sc), following the first row of the sequence below. For Rows 2–13, ch 1 (does not count), turn, and work each row's sequence.

1. Row 1 (RS): 14 B. (14 sc)
2. Row 2 (WS): 4 B, 2 A, 2 B, 2 A, 4 B. (14 sc)
3. Row 3 (RS): 3 B, 8 A, 3 B. (14 sc)
4. Row 4 (WS): 2 B, 10 A, 2 B. (14 sc)
5. Row 5 (RS): 2 B, 10 A, 2 B. (14 sc)
6. Row 6 (WS): 3 B, 8 A, 3 B. (14 sc)
7. Row 7 (RS): 4 B, 6 A, 4 B. (14 sc)
8. Row 8 (WS): 5 B, 4 A, 5 B. (14 sc)
9. Row 9 (RS): 2 B, 2 A, 6 B, 2 A, 2 B. (14 sc)
10. Row 10 (WS): 1 B, 4 A, 4 B, 4 A, 1 B. (14 sc)
11. Row 11 (RS): 1 B, 4 A, 4 B, 4 A, 1 B. (14 sc)
12. Row 12 (WS): 2 B, 2 A, 6 B, 2 A, 2 B. (14 sc)
13. Row 13 (RS): 14 B. (14 sc)

> [!PATTERN] Border
> With B, work sc evenly around, with 3 sc in each corner. Join. For a blanket, add rounds until every block measures the same.

## Pattern 15: Planning a C2C Name Banner

A guide for charting any name with the block alphabet (Chapter 59) and working it corner to corner.

1. **Width:** each letter is 5 tiles wide with 1 background tile between letters. A name of L letters needs 5L + (L − 1) tiles, plus a border of B tiles on each side.
2. **Height:** letters are 7 tiles tall; add B border tiles above and below.
3. **Example (arithmetic only):** "LUNA" (4 letters) with a 3-tile border: width 5 × 4 + 3 + 6 = 29 tiles; height 7 + 6 = 13 tiles. That is 29 × 13 = 377 tiles over 29 + 13 − 1 = 41 diagonals.
4. Chart the name on graph paper or a spreadsheet before you start, and count the tiles of each color.
5. Work the C2C rectangle (Chapter 61), following your chart along each diagonal.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Letters read backward on the wrong side
> Charts show the right side. If you work C2C with the wrong side facing for some rows, keep the chart the right way round and read each row in the working direction; don't flip the letters.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> A name in yarn is a gift that says "I thought only of you" for hours and hours.
