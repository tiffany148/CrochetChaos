Six more original patterns: two for the home, one to carry, one toy, and two quick wearables sized by your own measurements. As in the earlier portfolios, every count below was calculated and is asserted in the code that writes this chapter (`src/portfolio3.py`).

> [!STATUS] Testing status of every pattern in this chapter
> Written and calculated for this edition. **None has been test-crocheted, tech-edited, safety-tested or fit-tested.** Finished sizes are estimates that depend on your gauge. Work a swatch, and read the safety boxes before making a toy.

## Pattern 16: Hexagon Coaster Set

A flat hexagon in single crochet. Stacking the increases (placing each round's increase in the increase of the round below) makes six straight sides; this is the same arithmetic as a flat circle, 6 stitches more each round.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) cotton, which handles heat and absorbs drips; two colors |
| Hook | 4–4.5 mm |
| Notions | Tapestry needle, stitch marker |
| Finished size | Work until the coaster is the width you want; 7–8 rounds is a typical starting point at worsted gauge (estimate) |

> [!PATTERN] Coaster (main color)
> Rnd 1: Ch 1 (does not count), 6 sc in magic ring; join with sl st to first sc. (6)
> Rnd 2: Ch 1, 2 sc in each st around; join. (12)
> Rnd 3: Ch 1, (sc in next 1 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (18)
> Rnd 4: Ch 1, (sc in next 2 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (24)
> Rnd 5: Ch 1, (sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (30)
> Rnd 6: Ch 1, (sc in next 4 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (36)
> Rnd 7: Ch 1, (sc in next 5 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (42)
> Rnd 8: Ch 1, (sc in next 6 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (48)
> Fasten off main color.

> [!PATTERN] Border (contrast color)
> Join the contrast with a standing sc in any stitch. Sc in each st around, working 3 sc in the second sc of each of the six 2-sc increases of Rnd 8, and join with an invisible join. (48 + 12 = 60)

> [!HINT] Corner arithmetic
> Each corner gains 2 stitches (3 sc in one stitch), so the border adds 6 × 2 = 12 stitches to the 48 of Round 8.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Joined rounds drift
> The join of each round slowly moves to one side. Keep a marker in the first stitch and start each round there, and your corners stay where the count says.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Make coasters in sets of four from one color plus a contrast border each. They use up the little balls you could never bear to throw away.

## Pattern 17: Mesh Market Tote

A rectangular sc base, mesh sides worked in the round, and a solid sc band with two handle openings.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy+ |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) cotton or cotton blend |
| Hook | 5 mm |
| Notions | Stitch markers, tapestry needle |
| Base size | About 30 ÷ (your sc per inch) wide; for example, at 4 sc per inch the base is about 7.5 in (estimate) |

> [!PATTERN] Base
> Ch 31.
> Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (30)
> Rows 2–10: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (30)
> Do not fasten off.

> [!PATTERN] Sides
> Rnd 1: Ch 1, do not turn. Working around the base: sc in each st of Row 10 (30), 1 sc in each row end (10), sc in each ch of the foundation (30), 1 sc in each row end (10); join with sl st to first sc. (80)
> Rnd 2: Ch 4 (counts as dc and ch 1), sk next st, *dc in next st, ch 1, sk next st; rep from * around; join with sl st to 3rd ch of beginning ch-4. (40 dc and 40 ch-1 sps)
> Rnds 3–20: Sl st in first ch-1 sp, ch 4, *dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1; rep from * around; join. (40 dc and 40 sps)
> Rnd 21: Ch 1, sc in each dc and each ch-1 sp around; join. (80)
> Rnds 22–24: Ch 1, sc in each st around; join. (80)

> [!PATTERN] Handle round
> Place the join at the start of a long side. If needed, sl st along to get there.
> Rnd 25: Ch 1, [sc in next 8 sts, ch 40, sk next 14 sts, sc in next 8 sts, sc in next 10 sts] twice; join. (52 sc and two ch-40 loops)
> Rnd 26: Ch 1, [sc in next 8 sts, 40 sc in the ch-40 loop, sc in next 8 sts, sc in next 10 sts] twice; join. (132)
> Rnds 27–28: Ch 1, sc in each st around; join. (132)
> Fasten off and weave in the ends.

> [!HINT] Why the handle numbers work
> Rnd 1 has 80 stitches, 40 per half. Each half is 8 + 14 + 8 + 10 = 40. Replacing 14 skipped stitches with 40 sc on each side gives (8 + 40 + 8 + 10) × 2 = 132.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Cotton mesh stretches downward
> Mesh bags lengthen when loaded. Plan the sides a little shorter than you want them empty, and make the handles in solid sc, as here, so they do not stretch as much as the body.

> [!SAFETY] Load
> This tote has not been load-tested. Do not use it for heavy items until you have tested your own bag, and replace it if the handle joins start to stretch or fray.

## Pattern 18: Ollie the Curly Octopus (amigurumi)

A round head in spiral sc with eight curly legs. The legs curl because each chain gets two stitches.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy (amigurumi) |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) cotton or acrylic |
| Hook | 3.5–4 mm |
| Notions | Fiberfill, stitch marker, tapestry needle; embroidery yarn for eyes |

> [!PATTERN] Head and body (spiral rounds)
> Rnd 1: 6 sc in magic ring. (6)
> Rnd 2: Inc in each st around. (12)
> Rnd 3: (Sc in next st, inc) 6 times. (18)
> Rnd 4: (Sc in next 2 sts, inc) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 5: (Sc in next 3 sts, inc) 6 times. (30)
> Rnds 6–12: Sc in each st around. (30)
> Rnd 13: (Sc in next 3 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (24)
> Rnd 14: (Sc in next 2 sts, inv dec) 6 times. (18)
> Embroider the eyes now. Stuff firmly.
> Rnd 15: (Sc in next st, inv dec) 6 times. (12)
> Rnd 16: Inv dec 6 times. (6)
> Fasten off, close the hole, and leave the tail inside.

> [!PATTERN] Legs (make 8)
> Ch 16.
> Row 1: 2 sc in 2nd ch from hook and 2 sc in each ch across. (30)
> Fasten off, leaving a long tail. Sew the legs evenly around Rounds 13–15, two or three stitches apart.

> [!HINT] Leg math
> 16 chains leave 15 to work into after skipping the first. Two sc in each gives 30 stitches along the same length, so the strip must twist into a spiral.

> [!SAFETY] Toy safety
> This toy has not been safety-tested. Embroider the eyes rather than using safety eyes for very young children, sew each leg on securely, and check the seams regularly. Toys for children must meet the safety rules where they are sold.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Embroider faces before closing
> Eyes and mouths are far easier to place and tie off while you can still reach inside.

## Pattern 19: Linen Stitch Cowl

Worked flat in moss (linen) stitch from the Stitch Pattern Library, then seamed into a loop. Size is set by measuring, not by a printed chain count.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy |
| Yarn | DK to worsted (CYC 3–4), ideally a soft wool or wool blend; a variegated yarn shows off the woven look |
| Hook | One or two sizes larger than the label suggests, because moss stitch is firm |
| Notions | Tapestry needle |

> [!PATTERN] Cowl
> Make a swatch in moss stitch and measure stitches per inch.
> Chain an even number equal to about the cowl height you want × your stitches per inch (round to the nearest even number). For example, a 9 in height at 5 sts per inch needs about 45 → 46 ch.
> Rows: Work moss stitch (Stitch Pattern Library) until the piece reaches around your neck with the overlap you like, ending after a complete row.
> Finishing: Hold the short ends together and slip-stitch them closed. For an infinity cowl, give one end a half twist before seaming.

> [!HINT] Why measure instead of counting rows
> Cowl length is a personal choice and moss stitch gauge varies a lot between crocheters. Trying it on is more reliable than a row count.

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Moss stitch is where variegated yarns behave best. If a skein pooled into blotches in dc, try it here.

## Pattern 20: Ribbed Fingerless Mitts

A rectangle of back-loop sc rib worked sideways, folded and seamed with a gap for the thumb. All sizes come from your own hand.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy |
| Yarn | Worsted (CYC 4) wool or wool blend for warmth and stretch |
| Hook | 4.5–5 mm |
| Notions | Tapestry needle |

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p13-market-tote.jpg)

> [!PATTERN] Mitt (make 2)
> Measure: (a) the length from just below your knuckles to where you want the cuff to end, and (b) your hand circumference around the palm, below the thumb.
> Chain enough stitches to equal length (a), plus 1. Example: 7 in at 4 sts per inch = 28 sts, so ch 29.
> Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across.
> Row 2: Ch 1, turn. Sc in the back loop only of each st across.
> Rep Row 2 until the piece, gently stretched, wraps around measurement (b). Fasten off with a long tail.
> Seam: Fold the rectangle so the long edges meet. Sew from the top (knuckle) edge down for about 1 in, leave a gap for the thumb, then sew the rest of the seam to the cuff. Try the mitt on before you tie off; the thumb gap should sit at the base of your thumb.

> [!HINT] Which way the ribs run
> Because the rows run along the length of the mitt, the ridges circle your hand and stretch around it. That is why the rectangle is worked sideways.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Make the second mitt the same day
> Tension changes between sessions. Making both mitts close together, or working them two at a time, keeps them matched.

## Pattern 21: Chunky Round Basket

A sturdy basket for T-shirt yarn or a super bulky yarn: a flat sc base, a back-loop turning round, straight sides and two handle slots.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Skill level | Easy |
| Yarn | T-shirt yarn or super bulky (CYC 6–7) |
| Hook | 10–12 mm; choose a size that makes a stiff, closed fabric |
| Notions | Stitch markers, large-eye tapestry needle |

> [!PATTERN] Base
> Rnd 1: Ch 1 (does not count), 6 sc in magic ring; join with sl st to first sc. (6)
> Rnd 2: Ch 1, 2 sc in each st around; join. (12)
> Rnd 3: Ch 1, (sc in next 1 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (18)
> Rnd 4: Ch 1, (sc in next 2 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (24)
> Rnd 5: Ch 1, (sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (30)
> Rnd 6: Ch 1, (sc in next 4 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (36)
> Rnd 7: Ch 1, (sc in next 5 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (42)
> Rnd 8: Ch 1, (sc in next 6 sts, 2 sc in next st) 6 times; join. (48)

> [!PATTERN] Sides
> Rnd 1: Ch 1, sc in the back loop only of each st around; join. (48) This round makes the fold between base and side.
> Rnds 2–7: Ch 1, sc in each st around; join. (48)
> Rnd 8 (handle slots): Ch 1, [sc in next 9 sts, ch 6, sk next 6 sts, sc in next 9 sts] twice; join. (36 sc and two ch-6 spaces)
> Rnd 9: Ch 1, [sc in next 9 sts, 6 sc in ch-6 sp, sc in next 9 sts] twice; join. (48)
> Rnd 10: Ch 1, sl st in each st around (or reverse single crochet for a corded rim). Fasten off.

> [!HINT] Slot arithmetic
> 48 stitches ÷ 2 = 24 per side; 9 + 6 + 9 = 24. The slots sit opposite each other.

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Standard increases cup in bulky yarn
> Very thick yarn can make the base ripple or cup. If it does, add or skip an increase round to flatten it, and record the change. Because these increases stack, the base also tends toward a hexagon; for a rounder base, move the increase to the middle of the group on every other round (for example, Rnd 5: sc in next st, 2 sc in next st, sc in next 2 sts; repeat 6 times), which keeps the same count.

## How the portfolio counts were checked

Every number above comes from a small set of rules: flat circles and hexagons gain 6 stitches each round; mesh in dc with ch-1 uses 2 stitches per unit; and a handle loop replaces skipped stitches with the number of sc worked into it. The script that writes this chapter fails if any stated total does not match those rules.
