Patterns save space with abbreviations and charts with symbols. This chapter lists the abbreviations used in this book and in most English-language patterns, based on the Craft Yarn Council's standard list, with UK equivalents. Always read the abbreviations and the symbol key printed in your pattern, because designers may define special stitches their own way.

> [!HINT] Beginner version up front
> The Stitch & Abbreviation Dictionary at the front of this book lists the same abbreviations with a plain-English meaning for each one. The two lists use the same US and UK names.

## US and UK stitch names

The same words mean different stitches in the two systems. The UK system names a stitch by the number of yarn-overs plus one, so every name shifts up by one.

| US term | US abbr. | UK term | UK abbr. |
|---|---|---|---|
| chain | ch | chain | ch |
| slip stitch | sl st | slip stitch | ss |
| single crochet | sc | double crochet | dc |
| half double crochet | hdc | half treble | htr |
| double crochet | dc | treble | tr |
| treble crochet | tr | double treble | dtr |
| double treble | dtr | triple treble | ttr / trtr |
| triple treble | trtr | quadruple treble | qtr |
| skip | sk | miss | — |
| yarn over | yo | yarn over hook / yarn round hook | yoh / yrh |
| gauge | — | tension | — |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — Find the "sc" test
> To tell which system a pattern uses, look for single crochet (sc). Patterns written in UK terms do not use "single crochet". If the pattern has no sc and starts with lots of "dc" in a small amigurumi, it is probably UK.

## Punctuation in patterns

| Mark | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| * | Repeat from the asterisk as directed | *sc in next st, 2 sc in next st; rep from * around |
| [ ] or ( ) | Work the bracketed instructions as a group, the number of times stated, or all in one stitch/space | (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in corner sp |
| — or = at end | Stitch count at the end of the row or round | — 18 sts |
| ; | Separates steps within a repeat | |

> [!GRANNY] Granny's Wisdom
> Read a bracketed group out loud once before you start. "Three double, chain two, three double—all in the same space" is easier to remember than the punctuation.

## Chart symbols

The symbols below follow the conventions shown in the CYC chart-symbol list and Japanese-style charts. A chart's own key always takes priority.

![Decorative generated crochet art](../../assets/art/fill/p01-stash-shelf.jpg)

![symbol_key (schematic illustration)](../../assets/figures/symbol_key.png)

| Symbol shape | Stitch |
|---|---|
| Oval | chain |
| Filled dot | slip stitch |
| X or + | single crochet |
| T | half double crochet |
| T with one slash | double crochet |
| T with two slashes | treble crochet |
| Small arc drawn under a stitch symbol | front loop only or back loop only; the arc's direction distinguishes them, and charts differ, so check the key |
| Hooked stem at foot, curved forward | front post |
| Hooked stem at foot, curved back | back post |
| Several stitches meeting at the top | decrease or cluster |
| Several stitches fanning from one base | increase or shell |
| Small loop with dot | picot |

> [!DANG] Dang, I Wish I'd Known That! — The same symbol can mean X or +
> Single crochet appears as an X in many charts and as a + in others. Both mean the same stitch. It is not a different stitch.
