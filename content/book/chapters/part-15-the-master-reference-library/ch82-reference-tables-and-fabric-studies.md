## US and UK quick translation

| US term | UK equivalent | Construction reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Single crochet | Double crochet | Draw up loop, then draw through two |
| Half double crochet | Half treble crochet | One initial wrap, final draw through three |
| Double crochet | Treble crochet | One initial wrap, two successive two-loop closures |
| Treble crochet | Double treble crochet | Two initial wraps, three two-loop closures |
| Double treble crochet | Triple treble crochet | Three initial wraps, four two-loop closures |
| Gauge | Tension | Count over a stated measured span and treatment |
| Yarn over | Yarn over hook | Working movement, often yo or yoh |

Chain and slip stitch keep the same basic names. Special-stitch abbreviations vary. Translate the base construction and consult the actual definition rather than changing letters alone.

## Calculation reference

| Goal | Formula | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Stitch gauge | Stitches counted ÷ measured width | Same stitch definition and relaxed treatment |
| Row gauge | Rows counted ÷ measured length | Row convention stated |
| Raw width count | Target width × stitch gauge | Units must match |
| Planned width | Chosen count ÷ stitch gauge | Recalculate after rounding |
| Raw length count | Target length × row gauge | Shape and borders accounted for separately |
| Finished circumference | Body circumference + intended ease | Same location on body and garment |
| Two-stitch shaping events | Absolute net count change ÷ 2 | Only when each event changes total by two |
| Area estimate | Sample length × target area ÷ sample area | Comparable fabric; finishing added separately |
| Balls needed | Round up planning length ÷ length per ball | Use actual product length, consistent units |
| Three-position filet width | 3 × blocks + 1 | This book’s shared-boundary convention |
| Ideal circle diameter | Circumference ÷ pi | Geometric model, not verified crochet fit |

One inch is exactly 2.54 cm. One yard is exactly 0.9144 m. Keep conversion precision until the final practical rounding step. Do not treat 4 inches and 10 cm as exactly equal measurement windows.

## Fabric study 1 alternating heights

Ch 13. Row 1: sc in second chain, dc in next; repeat [sc in next chain, dc in next chain] five more times; 12 actual stitches. Row 2: ch 1, turn; [sc in next stitch, dc in next stitch] six times; 12 stitches. Repeat Row 2 through Row 8. The ch-1 never counts.

Each new sc is placed into a previous dc and each dc into a previous sc because the even-length row ends in dc before turning. This established height-alternation arrangement is often called lemon peel or griddle stitch, but naming varies. Record the sequence rather than relying on the nickname. Finish and compare both faces. Count consistency does not establish the fabric’s gauge or flatness in your yarn.

## Fabric study 2 narrow back-loop ribs

Ch 13. Row 1: sc in second chain and across; 12 sc. Rows 2–12: ch 1, turn; sc BLO in every stitch; 12 sc. Fasten off and secure ends. Mark the starting edge and the direction of the ridges.

Measure relaxed width and length after the intended care treatment. Gently extend across the ridges, release, and observe recovery. Compare that with extension along the ridges. The directions are not interchangeable when the fabric becomes a cuff. This sample investigates behavior; it does not approve a particular negative-ease percentage.

## Fabric study 3 window rows

Ch 14. Row 1: sc in second chain and across; 13 sc. Row 2: ch 1, turn; sc in first stitch, [ch 1, skip next stitch, sc in next stitch] six times. There are seven sc and six chain positions, totaling 13.

Row 3: ch 1, turn; sc in each actual sc and in each individual chain across; 13 sc. Repeat Rows 2–3 twice more, ending after Row 7. Fasten off and secure ends. The solid return row fills the chain positions but leaves the preceding window openings visible. Work into chain links rather than adding arbitrary stitches around a space for this indexed sample.

## Fabric study 4 V-group bands

Ch 18. Row 1: sc in second chain and across; 17 sc. Row 2: ch 2, turn; the chain does not count. Dc in first stitch, then repeat [skip next stitch, (dc, ch 1, dc) in next stitch, skip next stitch, dc in next stitch] four times.

Each repeat consumes four old stitches and creates four positions: two dc, one chain, and the final dc. With the initial dc, the row has 17 positions. Row 3: ch 1, turn; sc in each dc and in each individual ch-1 across; 17 sc. Repeat Rows 2–3 twice more. This is a banded V arrangement with solid return rows, not an unspecified universal V-stitch fabric. Physical spreading and edge behavior remain to be sampled.

## Fabric study 5 bobble columns

Use the Chapter 20 five-dc bobble definition with one shared top and no additional chain. Ch 16. Row 1: sc in second chain and across; 15 sc. Row 2: ch 1, turn; sc in first 2 sts, bobble in next st, [sc in next 4 sts, bobble in next st] twice, sc in final 2 sts; 15 units.

Row 3: ch 1, turn; sc in each actual sc and each marked bobble top; 15 sc. Repeat Rows 2–3 twice more. Mark the top of every bobble on the first textured row so its next-row destination is clear. Inspect which face carries the bumps and record the intended RS. This sample uses the book’s definition rather than assuming every publisher’s bobble has the same closure.

## Fabric study 6 post-stitch blocks

Ch 14. Row 1: dc in third chain from hook and each chain across; 12 actual dc. The skipped two chains do not count. Row 2: ch 2, turn; [FPdc around next 3 posts, BPdc around next 3 posts] twice; 12 post stitches. Row 3: repeat Row 2.

Row 4: ch 2, turn; [BPdc around next 3 posts, FPdc around next 3 posts] twice. Row 5: repeat Row 4. Repeat Rows 2–5 once more, ending at Row 9. Do not also work into the top V associated with a post already used. The arrangement produces a block-texture investigation; final relief and dimensions require a physical sample. The repeated order after turning is deliberate for an even number of alternating three- post groups.

## Fabric study 7 three heights in bands

Ch 13. Rows 1–3: work 12 sc per row, beginning Row 1 in second chain and using a non-counting ch-1 thereafter. Rows 4–6: ch 2, turn, hdc in each of 12 actual stitches; beginning chain never counts. Rows 7– 9: ch 2, turn, dc in each actual stitch; 12 dc.

Finish and label the three regions. They have equal stitch counts, not guaranteed equal widths. Their heights will also differ. Measure the bands separately after care and compare the transitions. A design using those bands needs a composite sample, not a gauge borrowed from only the single crochet region.

## Fabric study 8 grouped and plain rows

Ch 20 and work 19 sc in Row 1 from the second chain. Row 2: ch 1, turn; sc in first stitch; repeat [skip 2 stitches, 5 dc in next stitch, skip 2 stitches, sc in next stitch] three times. There are four sc and 15 dc, or 19 actual stitches.

Row 3: ch 1, turn; sc in each actual stitch; 19 sc. Repeat Rows 2–3 twice more. This extends the Chapter 20 shell sample into repeated bands without changing its count convention. Inspect whether the particular yarn spreads, cups, or forms a stable fabric. The arithmetic keeps 19 stitches; it does not guarantee a flat rectangle.

## Sample record minimum

For every study, retain the yarn declaration, hook, counts, entry and turning conventions, treatment, measured gauge, observed dimensions, and a photo of each face. Label an unmade or untested study accurately. A table of checked counts is useful evidence, but a sample record becomes a physical test only when the sample has actually been made and observed.
