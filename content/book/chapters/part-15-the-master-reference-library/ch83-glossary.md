Definitions in this glossary use US terms; UK terms are given where they differ. For abbreviations see Chapter 81; for how to work each stitch see the Stitch Encyclopedia, Chapter 22.

## A

**Amigurumi.** Small stuffed crocheted toys or figures, usually worked in tight single crochet spirals.

## B

**Back bar.** The horizontal bar behind the top V of a stitch (especially hdc); also the bump on the back of a chain.

**Back loop.** The loop of the top V farthest from you.

**Blocking.** Wetting, steaming or spraying finished crochet and pinning it to shape to dry.

**Bobbin.** A small holder or butterfly of yarn for one color area in C2C or intarsia work.

**Bobble.** A group of incomplete tall stitches worked in one stitch and closed together so they pop out of the fabric.

**Border.** Rounds or rows worked around the outside of a finished piece.

**Bruges lace.** Lace made from a narrow crocheted tape that is turned and joined to itself.

## C

**Ch-sp.** Chain space; see Chain space.

**Chain space (ch-sp).** The gap under one or more chains, worked into rather than into the chains themselves.

**Chainless foundation.** See Foundation stitches.

**Chart.** A drawing of a pattern using symbols (stitch chart) or colored squares (color chart).

**Cluster.** Several partial stitches worked into one or more stitches and finished together at the top.

**Colorwork.** Any technique that places colors in a pattern: stripes, tapestry, mosaic, C2C, intarsia.

**Corner-to-corner (C2C).** A method built from small diagonal tiles, often used for pixel pictures.

**Crab stitch.** Reverse single crochet, worked in the opposite direction to give a corded edge.

## D

**Dc2tog.** Double crochet two together, a one-stitch decrease (UK: tr2tog).

**Decrease.** Reducing the stitch count, usually by working stitches together.

**Drape.** How a fabric hangs and flows.

**Dye lot.** A batch of yarn dyed together; lots can differ slightly in color.

## E

**Ease.** The difference between a body measurement and the finished garment measurement. Negative ease means the item is smaller than the body.

**Edging.** A narrow decorative border worked along an edge.

## F

**Fasten off.** Cut the yarn and pull the tail through the last loop to secure it.

**Felting.** Shrinking and matting animal-fiber fabric with heat, moisture and agitation.

**Filet crochet.** A mesh of open and filled squares that forms pictures or letters.

**Floats.** Strands of unused color carried across the back of the work (in crochet, usually hidden inside stitches instead).

**Foundation chain.** The chain that starts a piece worked in rows.

**Foundation stitches.** Stitches that create the chain and first row at the same time (fsc, fdc).

**Frogging.** Pulling out stitches to undo work (rip-it, rip-it).

**Front loop.** The loop of the top V nearest to you.

## G

**Gauge.** The number of stitches and rows in a measured area; UK: tension.

**Gauge swatch.** A sample worked to measure gauge; see Swatch.

**Granny square.** A square motif worked in rounds of clusters, traditionally three dc, separated by chain spaces.

## H

**Hairpin lace.** Lace strips made on a two-pronged loom with a crochet hook.

**Hank.** Yarn wound in a large loop and twisted; wind it into a ball before use.

## I

**Increase.** Adding stitches, usually by working more than one stitch into the same place.

**Intarsia.** Colorwork with separate yarn sections for each color block, not carried across.

**Invisible decrease.** A front-loops decrease used in amigurumi to hide decreases.

**Invisible join.** Finishing a round by sewing the tail through the next stitch to imitate a stitch top.

**Irish crochet.** Lace made of separately worked dimensional motifs joined by a mesh background.

## J

**Join.** To connect the end of a round to its beginning (usually with a sl st), or to connect pieces.

## L

**Live loop.** The working loop on the hook; never counted as a stitch.

## M

**Magic knot.** A yarn join made of two overhand knots, each tied around the other yarn, pulled together and trimmed; no ends to weave, but it leaves a small knot (see the joining chapter).

**Magic ring.** An adjustable loop that lets the center of a round piece close completely; also magic circle.

**Mattress stitch.** A sewn seam worked from the right side that is nearly invisible.

**Mosaic crochet.** Colorwork worked one color per row, using dropped stitches into lower rows to place color.

**Motif.** A single shaped unit (square, hexagon, flower) that is joined to others.

## N

**Negative ease.** A finished measurement smaller than the body measurement, so the item stretches to fit.

## P

**Picot.** A small chain loop, closed with a slip stitch, used as a decorative bump.

**Ply.** One of the strands twisted together to make a yarn.

**Popcorn.** A group of complete stitches closed together by drawing a loop through the first stitch.

**Post.** The vertical body of a stitch.

**Puff stitch.** Several loops drawn up in one stitch and closed together.

## R

**Repeat.** A section of instructions or chart worked more than once.

**Ribbing.** Stretchy fabric of alternating raised and recessed columns or ridges, used for bands and cuffs.

**Right side (RS).** The side of the fabric meant to be seen.

**Round.** A circuit of stitches worked around a center or tube.

**Row.** A line of stitches worked back and forth; the work is turned at each end.

**Russian join.** A knotless yarn join: each tail is threaded back through its own strand so the two loops interlock.

## S

**Sc2tog.** Single crochet two together, a one-stitch decrease (UK: dc2tog).

**Scrumble.** A small freeform patch of mixed stitches and textures.

**Shell.** Several stitches worked into the same stitch or space, fanning outward.

**Short rows.** Partial rows worked to add depth in one area of a piece.

**Skein.** A twisted or wound package of yarn; also ball, hank, cake.

**Slip knot.** An adjustable knot that puts the first loop on the hook.

**Spike stitch.** A stitch worked into a row below the current one, leaving a long stem.

**Spiral.** Rounds worked continuously without joining; common in amigurumi.

**Spit splice.** A knotless join for feltable animal fibers: overlapped ends are moistened and rubbed until they felt together. Also called a felted join.

**Stitch marker.** A removable clip or ring placed in a stitch to mark a position.

**Stitch multiple.** The number of stitches needed for one repeat of a pattern, often with extras for edges (e.g., multiple of 6 + 2).

**Superwash.** Wool treated to resist felting so it can be machine washed.

**Swatch.** A test piece used to measure gauge and preview fabric.

## T

**Tail.** The cut end of the yarn at the start or end of work.

**Tapestry crochet.** Colorwork in which unused colors are carried inside the stitches.

**Tech editor.** A reviewer who checks a pattern's math, consistency and clarity.

**Tension (yarn).** How tightly you hold the yarn; UK patterns also use tension to mean gauge.

**Top V.** The two loops at the top of a stitch, shaped like a sideways V.

**Tunisian crochet.** A crochet method in which loops are collected on a long hook in a forward pass and worked off in a return pass.

**Turning chain.** Chains made at the start of a row to raise the hook to the height of the stitches.

## V

**Variegated yarn.** Yarn dyed with several colors along its length.

## W

**Weaving in.** Securing yarn tails by threading them through stitches on the wrong side.

**Whipstitch.** A simple overcast sewn seam.

**Working yarn.** The yarn coming from the ball, as opposed to the tail.

**WPI (wraps per inch).** The number of times a yarn wraps side by side in an inch; used to estimate weight.

**Wrong side (WS).** The side not meant to be seen.

## Y

**Yardage.** The length of yarn in a ball or needed for a project.

**Yarn over (yo).** Wrapping the yarn over the hook from back to front; UK: yarn round hook.
